Four ways into the phone

Same content, same destinations, four different gestures for getting between the lock and the four modes. Try each one with a thumb (mouse-drag works too).

Directions are fixed across all four: ↑ Discover · ↓ Ask · ← Connect · → Home. Once committed, tap "reset" to return to the lock screen.

Mocks render in the browser with full interactivity — drag and release to commit. The threshold to commit is ~64px for variants B and C; variant A uses the existing puck-in-ring pattern. None of the destinations are themselves interactive in this mock; they're representative glimpses pulled from the Argos 2026 source.

Variant A · current

Puck in a ring

Press the center puck. Drag toward a direction. Release past the ring's edge to commit. The pattern as built today.

What came from where

From A · puck

Fingerprint as the resting state.

The Pixel pattern: a circle on the lock screen showing where to put your thumb, with a fingerprint icon inside. Always visible, gently breathing at rest. Solves "the user opens the phone and nothing tells them where to start." The whole gesture begins from here.

From C · cross-dissolve

Destination fades in during drag.

Smoothstep curve over 74px of drag. Lock dims to 50% as you go. Lets you back out mid-gesture and reconsider — partial drag shows partial destination, not a hard binary.

From D · mood lighting

Each room has its own light.

A subtle colored bleed grows from the swipe-direction edge — Discover cool blue (top), Ask amber (bottom), Connect rose (left), Home pale daylight (right). After commit, the destination's own mood-tinted background carries the light forward. Palette dialed down ~35% from D — should read as atmosphere, not theme.

From B · drag-from-anywhere, repurposed

Destination name as a quiet preview.

Instead of the four-direction labels around a ring, a single label fades in at the top-left during drag — "↑ Discover" — naming where you're going. The corner is the right place because it stays out of the dissolve's way.

Open dials

Breath rate, mood intensity, threshold.

Anchor sized to Pixel 8 default (48dp circle, 22dp icon) at ~25% from bottom. Breath: 2.6s ease-in-out at ±12% scale. Anchor drifts up to 16px with the thumb during drag. Mood tints sit at 6–11% on the destination background and bleed at 14–26% during drag. Commit threshold is 70px. All easy to tune once you've held it on a real thumb.

Alt register · for fun

If Rauno designed this

Same gesture, same destinations. But the visual register shifts: typography-led, monospace metadata, hairlines instead of glows, a coordinate-target instead of a fingerprint sensor, a single drawn line instead of a mood bleed, indexed mode labels as the compass.

The interaction is identical to the Refined version — same fingerprint-anchor metaphor, same four-direction commit at 70px, same destinations behind. What changes is how the screen talks.

Moves pulled from the lineage

Standard Android chrome at top. The status bar stays normal — real time, notification icons, wifi, signal, battery percent, battery icon. The Rauno register is in the body of the screen, not in the OS chrome at the edge. Familiar ground above the typographic monolith.

Mono as a co-star in the body. Notifications, mode labels, and the time-meta line are all in JetBrains Mono with tight letter-spacing. The big time is sans (Inter Light, weight 200). The contrast between the two types — sans for the moment, mono for the metadata — is the design language.

The four modes as a compass. DISCOVER at the top edge, HOME right, ASK down, CONNECT left — clockwise from the top. The active mode tightens its letter-spacing from 0.20em to 0.24em, which reads as "selected" without color.

Hairlines, not glows. Three thin rules frame the time block (above, mid, below). During drag, a single 0.6px line draws from the anchor toward the active direction — a precise vector, not a mood. The mood-bleed gets dropped entirely.

Coordinate-target, not sensor indicator. The fingerprint becomes a tiny crosshair-with-corner-brackets. Reads as instrument framing rather than biometric UI. The biometric still works underneath — the visual is just dressed differently.

Weather as a glyph. A small line-art partly-cloudy icon next to the time at ~⅓ its height. Carries the technical/instrument register through to the meteorological detail. The temperature and condition still appear in the meta line below — the icon is glanceable redundancy, like the gauge next to the digit.

Flashlight and camera as glyphs in the corners. Same line-art treatment, low opacity at rest, brightening on hover. The standard Pixel utility affordances, drawn in the Rauno hand.

Subtraction of warmth. Pure #000 background. Pure white type at varying opacity. No amber, no rose, no near-black — Rauno's register is cooler than ours by design. Worth knowing what we'd lose if we shipped this (the calming warmth of PTC) and what we'd gain (the precision-instrument register that signals "this is a different kind of phone").

What it's still missing

The four modes don't feel different from each other after commit. The mood lighting was doing real work to give Discover a different atmosphere than Connect. Without it, the four rooms become typographically distinct but tonally identical. If this register ships, we'll want to find Rauno's equivalent of mood — likely a per-mode accent color used very narrowly (a single 1px rule turning a specific hue), or per-mode typographic treatment.

What's in the mock

  • Lock-screen content matches Argos 2026 source: time, date, weather (☀ 68°), two seeded notifications (Sarah/Messages, Slack), Android status bar.
  • Destination glimpses pulled from the same source: Discover shows the 5 default scroll apps with their grayscale-mode treatment; Connect shows real contact/platform pairs (Sarah/Marcus/Mom/Dev Group/Jamie); Ask shows the waveform + memory snippet pattern; Home shows the app grid + 4-app dock (Phone/Messages/Chrome/Camera).
  • Thresholds: 60px in A (committed only after the puck crosses the ring's outer edge), 64px in B, 74px in C and D.
  • Mood palette in D — Discover: late-night library blue (top). Ask: candlelit amber (bottom). Connect: golden-hour rose (left). Home: cool midday daylight (right, gentlest of the four because Home is the baseline). All tints sit at 10–18% opacity over near-black — restraint over saturation.

Open questions

  • Cross-dissolve curve in C is currently linear-ish. Easing it (slow start, faster at threshold) might land more elegantly — let me know which the thumb prefers.
  • The current mock fully commits or fully snaps back. A third state — "you started but stopped halfway" — could either snap back (cheap, decisive) or hold position (information-rich but probably indecisive). Default is snap.
  • Variant B has a quiet "drag in any direction" hint at the bottom. Probably too obvious for production; useful in the mock so you can find the affordance.
