# Comic-style image prompts — Phone That Cares

Drafted 2026-04-25 for use with gpt-image-1 (or comparable). Four visuals × three styles = 12 prompts. Each prompt is intentionally exhaustive — image models infer almost nothing.

**Styles:**
- **A — Risograph:** fluorescent two-color inks, grain, slight misregistration, zine/political-poster register
- **B — gpt-image-1 default:** full-color editorial comic, painterly, NYT Magazine register
- **C — Minimal line drawing + 1–2 accent colors:** Christoph Niemann / New Yorker register

---

## Visual 1 — Toll Booth / Money Flows

**Concept:** The digital economy quietly extracts a tax that the physical economy doesn't. When you buy coffee, ~97% of the money goes to the coffee shop. When you buy an app, 30% goes to Apple's toll booth. And Google pays Apple ~$20B/year just to be the default search engine — money flowing between the two companies that supposedly compete to make your phone.

### 1A — Risograph

A two-color risograph print in the style of a political activism poster, fluorescent pink and teal inks on cream paper, with visible grain, slight ink misregistration, and the soft halftone dot patterns characteristic of risograph printing. Composition: a wide horizontal scene split into two stacked rows. TOP ROW labeled "PHYSICAL ECONOMY" in bold sans-serif: a person hands cash to a smiling coffee shop barista; a thick teal arrow labeled "$5" goes from customer to barista, and a thin pink arrow labeled "≈3% card fee" branches off to a tiny bank icon in the corner. BOTTOM ROW labeled "DIGITAL ECONOMY" in bold sans-serif: the same person taps their phone to buy an app from a small indie developer drawn as a tired figure at a laptop; but between them stands a massive ominous toll booth labeled "APP STORE" with the Apple logo on top, and a uniformed toll-booth attendant in a pink cap holding out a hand. A teal arrow labeled "$5" goes from customer into the toll booth; a pink arrow labeled "$1.50 (30%)" goes into Apple's pocket; a smaller teal arrow labeled "$3.50" continues to the developer. To the right side, separated by a dotted line, a side scene: a Google logo character hands a giant pink check labeled "$20 BILLION / YEAR" to an Apple logo character; small caption beneath reads "to be the default search." Overall mood: punk zine, indignant, clear, slightly humorous. Heavy black outlines, flat color fills, no gradients. Hand-lettered feel for all labels.

### 1B — gpt-image-1 default (editorial comic)

A full-color editorial illustration in the style of a modern New York Times opinion-section comic — painterly digital art with soft shading, warm and slightly muted palette, expressive characters with rounded features. Format: a three-panel horizontal comic strip with thin black borders between panels. PANEL 1 (titled "Buying Coffee"): A young woman in a yellow raincoat hands a $5 bill across a wooden counter to a friendly barista in a green apron. A simple arrow labeled "$5" curves from her hand to his. He's smiling. Warm café interior, plants in the window. PANEL 2 (titled "Buying an App"): The same woman, now sitting on a couch, taps her phone. Between her phone and the screen of an indie developer (a tired-looking person in a hoodie at a cluttered desk in a small apartment) sits a comically oversized brick-and-mortar TOLL BOOTH with a striped barrier arm and a sign reading "APP STORE — 30% TOLL." A uniformed Apple-logo character leans out the toll booth window, palm extended, taking $1.50 of her $5. The developer receives the remaining $3.50 with a shrug. PANEL 3 (titled "The Backroom Deal"): A dimly lit boardroom. A Google-logo character in a suit slides an enormous novelty check across a mahogany table to an Apple-logo character. The check reads "TWENTY BILLION DOLLARS" with the memo line "to be the default search engine on iPhone." Both characters look pleased; the viewer sees them through a slightly cracked door, like we weren't meant to see this. Expressive, warm, slightly satirical. Detailed but not cluttered. All labels and dollar amounts must appear exactly as written.

### 1C — Minimal line drawing with one accent color (warning red)

A minimalist editorial-style line drawing in the visual register of Christoph Niemann or a New Yorker spot illustration: clean, thin black ink lines on a white background, no shading, no texture, generous negative space. One single accent color used sparingly: a saturated warning red. Composition: a single horizontal scene, three vignettes connected by a thin black line representing money flow. LEFT VIGNETTE: A simple line-drawn person hands a coin to a simple line-drawn coffee cup with a face. The coin and the small "$5" label above it are in red. The arrow continues to the right. CENTER VIGNETTE: A literal toll booth — a small striped boom barrier, a tiny attendant booth — drawn entirely in black line, except the striped barrier arm and the words "APP STORE: 30%" above it are in red. The flow line passes through the toll booth and continues thinner, with a small "$3.50" label in black on the far side. RIGHT VIGNETTE: Two simple geometric figures — one labeled "GOOGLE," one labeled "APPLE" — with a thick red arrow between them labeled "$20B/yr → default search." Below the entire scene, in small black hand-lettered serif: *"One of these economies has a toll booth. Guess which one builds the road."* Restrained, witty, sophisticated. Lots of white space. Lines should feel hand-drawn but confident, slight wobble.

## Visual 2 — Body While Scrolling

**Concept:** A person hunched over their phone, with seven body systems quietly suffering — dopamine system fried, cortisol elevated, eyes strained, heart rate elevated, neck strained, thumb cramping, circadian rhythm disrupted. Each part has a "voice" — what it would say if it could speak.

### 2A — Risograph

A two-color risograph illustration in the style of a vintage medical poster crossed with a punk zine, printed in fluorescent orange and deep blue inks on off-white paper, with heavy grain, halftone dots, and slight ink misregistration. Center of composition: a side-profile of a young person sitting on the edge of a bed in a darkened bedroom, hunched dramatically over a glowing phone — the spine curved into a "C," neck jutting forward, shoulders rounded, thumb mid-scroll. The phone screen glows orange and is the brightest point in the image. The figure is rendered mostly in deep blue line work with orange spot color highlighting tense areas (the strained neck, the cramping thumb, the wide pupils). Surrounding the figure, like an old anatomical chart, are seven labeled callouts with thin lines pointing to body parts, each containing a hand-lettered "voice" in italic: from the eyes — *"It's been four hours since I blinked properly"*; from the neck — *"I'm holding up a bowling ball at the wrong angle"*; from the thumb — *"I've swiped 1,200 times today"*; from the chest/heart — *"My rhythm is off — every notification is a tiny adrenaline shot"*; from the brain — *"Dopamine's tapped out. Show me something newer"*; from the adrenal glands (lower back) — *"Cortisol still running. It's almost midnight"*; from a small moon icon outside the figure — *"Your circadian rhythm called. It's lost."* Title across the top in large hand-lettered block letters: "WHAT YOUR BODY IS SAYING WHILE YOU SCROLL." Mood: tender, slightly sad, conspiratorial — like the body is whispering to the reader behind the user's back. Heavy outlines, flat fills, riso texture throughout.

### 2B — gpt-image-1 default (editorial comic)

A full-color, painterly editorial illustration in the style of a New York Times Magazine feature opener — soft warm-cool contrast, gentle digital painting with visible brush texture, expressive but restrained character work. A young person of ambiguous ethnicity, mid-20s, in a soft gray t-shirt and pajama pants, sits hunched on the edge of an unmade bed in a dim bedroom, late at night. Their face is lit pale blue by the glow of the phone in their hands; the rest of the room falls into warm shadow. Posture is exaggerated but believable: shoulders rolled forward, neck craned down, spine curved, thumb mid-swipe. Their expression is blank — not sad, not happy, just absent. Around them, drawn in a slightly lighter, more illustrative register — like dream-thoughts or x-ray overlays — are seven small "voice bubbles" coming from different parts of their body, each containing italicized text in a hand-lettered font: from the eyes (*"Four hours since I blinked"*); from the neck (*"Holding up a bowling ball at the wrong angle"*); from the thumb (*"1,200 swipes today"*); over the chest, where a faintly visible heart pulses (*"Every ping is an adrenaline shot"*); from the head, with a faint radiating diagram (*"Dopamine system says: tapped out"*); from the lower back (*"Cortisol still running. It's almost midnight"*); and from a small moon visible through the bedroom window (*"Your circadian rhythm called. It's lost."*). The voices are gentle, almost loving — like the body is patiently trying to get the person's attention. The phone screen shows a generic infinite-feed pattern, the brightest point in the image. Mood: tender, melancholy, intimate. All voice text must appear exactly as quoted.

### 2C — Minimal line drawing with two accent colors (soft pink + warm yellow)

A minimalist line drawing in the spirit of an old-school anatomical diagram crossed with a contemporary editorial spot illustration. Clean, confident black ink lines on white background. Two accent colors only: a soft dusty pink (used for body/skin tone where it appears) and a warm sunset yellow (used only for the phone glow). No shading, no texture, no gradient — flat colors only. A line-drawn figure in side profile, simplified almost to a pictogram but anatomically expressive: hunched on the edge of a simple bed (a single horizontal line), curved spine clearly visible, neck jutting forward, thumb on phone. The phone is the only object glowing yellow, and the yellow softly tints the face. Around the figure, like labels on an antique anatomy chart, seven thin black lines extend outward to small handwritten serif labels in black ink. Each label has a body part name in small caps and an italicized voice underneath. Exactly these seven, in this order: EYES — *"four hours since I blinked"*; NECK — *"a bowling ball, held at the wrong angle"*; THUMB — *"1,200 swipes"*; HEART — *"every ping, a tiny adrenaline shot"*; BRAIN — *"dopamine: tapped out"*; ADRENALS — *"cortisol still running"*; CIRCADIAN — *"i don't know what time means anymore."* Generous white space around the figure. The image should feel quiet, diagnostic, slightly poetic — as if a doctor wrote a love note about the body. Restrained, sophisticated.

## Visual 3 — Perceptual Layer

**Concept:** A proposed alternative. Between the addictive content pipeline and the user's eyes/attention sits a "perceptual layer" — a software layer that introduces friction, triages notifications, applies grayscale, surfaces time awareness, and asks for user intent. The "before" is a firehose; the "after" is filtered.

### 3A — Risograph

A two-color risograph print in the style of a 1970s technical manual diagram crossed with a punk infographic poster. Inks: fluorescent green (for the perceptual layer) and deep purple-black (for everything else), printed on warm off-white paper. Heavy grain, halftone dots, slight misregistration. Composition: a horizontal three-zone diagram, left to right. LEFT ZONE labeled "THE PIPELINE": a chaotic tangle of icons — TikTok-style infinite scroll, news headlines, push notifications, ad banners, autoplay video previews — all rendered in dense purple-black line work, with arrows pointing right, conveying overwhelming velocity. Small caption beneath: *"engineered to capture."* CENTER ZONE labeled "PERCEPTUAL LAYER" in large hand-lettered block letters: a vertical band of bright fluorescent green stretching from top to bottom, drawn like a translucent membrane or filter. Inside the green band, five labeled mechanisms shown as small icons: a slowing-down clock ("FRICTION"), a sorting funnel ("NOTIFICATION TRIAGE"), a color-drained square ("GRAYSCALE"), an hourglass ("TIME AWARENESS"), a question mark in a thought bubble ("INTENT CHECK"). RIGHT ZONE labeled "YOU": a single calm human eye, looking forward, drawn in clean purple-black line. Caption beneath: *"what actually reaches your attention."* Across the bottom of the image, a before/after comparison: LEFT — a hand holding a phone with a cluttered chaotic screen rendered in dense purple-black; RIGHT — the same hand holding a phone with a calm, sparse screen showing only one or two purposeful items, with a soft green tint. Mood: hopeful, manifesto-like, "what if it could be different." Hand-lettered headlines, flat color fills.

### 3B — gpt-image-1 default (editorial comic)

A two-panel full-color editorial comic in the style of a modern explainer illustration — clean digital painting, warm humane palette, expressive but legible. PANEL 1, titled "TODAY," shows the phone experience as it currently is: a person sits on a park bench, but their face is overwhelmed, eyes wide, slightly frantic. From their phone, a literal firehose erupts — a chaotic stream of notifications, video thumbnails, news alerts, ads, trending headlines, all spraying into their face. The torrent is rendered in saturated reds, oranges, and electric blues. The person's body language is rigid, shoulders up. PANEL 2, titled "WITH A PERCEPTUAL LAYER," shows the same person on the same bench, but now between the phone and their face is a softly glowing translucent green pane of glass — the "perceptual layer" — labeled clearly. The same firehose hits the green pane, but the pane filters it: most of the chaos splatters and runs down the outside of the pane like rain on a window. What passes through to the person is a small, calm, ordered set of just three things — labeled in tiny text: a message from a friend, a calendar reminder, one chosen news article. The person's face is relaxed, eyes soft, slight smile. They look like themselves. On the green pane itself, five small icons are visible: a friction symbol (a hand raised "wait"), a triage funnel, a grayscale swatch, a clock, and a thought-bubble question mark — each with a tiny caption: "friction," "triage," "grayscale," "time awareness," "intent check." Mood: hopeful, demonstrative, the visual equivalent of "imagine if." Both panels share consistent color logic: the chaos is hot-colored, the filtered version is cool-greens and warm skin tones.

### 3C — Minimal line drawing with one accent color (calm green)

A minimalist editorial diagram-illustration in the spirit of Christoph Niemann or a Pentagram poster: clean black ink lines on white, generous negative space, no shading, no texture. Single accent color: a calm sage green, used only for the "perceptual layer" element. Composition: a horizontal flow with three labeled regions, left to right. LEFT, labeled simply "THE PIPELINE" in small black sans-serif caps: a dense scribble of overlapping lines and tiny icons (notification dots, video play triangles, dollar signs, hearts) — drawn in fine black line work, suggesting overwhelming chaos through density alone. CENTER, labeled "PERCEPTUAL LAYER" in small black sans-serif caps: a single tall vertical rectangle, filled flat in calm sage green — like a pane of frosted glass. Inside the green pane, five small black line icons stacked vertically with tiny black labels: a slowing clock ("friction"), a funnel ("triage"), a circle half-filled ("grayscale"), a clock face ("time"), a question mark ("intent"). RIGHT, labeled "YOU" in small black sans-serif caps: a single line-drawn human eye, calm, looking left at the green pane. The chaotic scribble on the left has many thin arrows pointing toward the green pane; from the green pane, only three thin clean arrows continue to the eye. Below the entire diagram, in small italic black serif: *"what if the phone had a layer that worked for you?"* Restrained, intelligent, hopeful. The kind of image you'd see in a thoughtful magazine essay.

## Visual 4 — Content Pipeline / Phone as Chokepoint

**Concept:** Creators make content. Platforms package it. Algorithms select and accelerate it. All of that funnels through one chokepoint — the phone — before reaching your eyes and attention. The phone is the only player in the chain with both the technical means and the trust-position to restrict the flow, but it's also the player whose business model benefits from the flow being maximal. So nothing narrows.

### 4A — Risograph

A two-color risograph print in the style of a 1970s political infographic poster crossed with an underground comic. Inks: fluorescent red and deep navy blue on cream paper, with heavy grain, halftone dots, and slight ink misregistration throughout. Composition: a horizontal pipeline diagram drawn as a literal industrial pipe system, viewed from the side, running left to right across the entire image. FAR LEFT — labeled "CREATORS" in bold hand-lettered block caps: a chaotic crowd of small human figures (a teenager filming a dance, a journalist at a laptop, a streamer with a ring light, a comedian at a mic, an outraged podcaster, a brand marketer with a clipboard) all shoving content — drawn as little red rectangles labeled "video," "post," "story," "ad," "rant" — into the wide-open mouth of a giant funnel. The funnel is overflowing; some content spills onto the floor. NEXT SECTION — labeled "PLATFORMS": the funnel narrows into a pipe that passes through three industrial-looking machines stamped "TIKTOK," "INSTAGRAM," "YOUTUBE," each compressing and packaging the red content rectangles. NEXT SECTION — labeled "ALGORITHM": the pipe enters a sinister-looking black box with dials, sparks, and small navy-blue arrows showing some content getting boosted (large) and some getting suppressed (small), with a tiny figure in a lab coat tweaking knobs. NEXT SECTION — and this is the visual climax — labeled in the largest hand-lettered text in the image, "THE PHONE": the pipe enters a cartoonishly small opening in the side of an enormous smartphone drawn in deep navy blue line work, taking up the vertical center of the composition. The opening is barely the width of a single content rectangle — a bottleneck — but the pipe upstream is gushing. Content is backed up, piling, pressurized, some squeezing through, some bursting out the seams. Sitting on top of the phone, smiling and waving, is a small mascot character drawn as a friendly Apple-or-Google-logo hybrid wearing a hard hat and holding a wrench, captioned in a small speech bubble: *"I could narrow this opening anytime. I won't."* FAR RIGHT — labeled "YOUR EYES" in small caps: a single tired human eye receiving the firehose that does make it through, pupils dilated. Beneath the entire pipeline, a single hand-lettered line of text reads: *"every player upstream is paid to push more. only one player could push less. guess what they're paid for."* Mood: indignant, slightly absurd, conspiratorial, like a propaganda poster on behalf of the user. Heavy outlines, flat color fills, hand-lettered labels throughout, riso grain visible everywhere.

### 4B — gpt-image-1 default (editorial comic)

A single-panel full-color editorial illustration in the style of a New York Times Magazine feature illustration or a Tom Gauld comic spread — painterly digital art, warm humane palette with strategic use of saturated reds for urgency, expressive characters, slightly satirical tone. Wide horizontal composition reading left to right as a literal supply chain. FAR LEFT: a chaotic, joyful, exhausting crowd of content creators, each one a distinct character — a Gen-Z teenager filming themselves dancing in a bedroom, a serious journalist hunched at a laptop in a newsroom, a livestreamer surrounded by ring lights, a stand-up comedian at a mic, a yoga influencer in athleisure, an angry political podcaster gesturing at a microphone, a corporate brand-marketer in a blazer with a clipboard. They are all enthusiastically tossing their work — drawn as little glowing rectangles — into a massive open funnel. CENTER-LEFT: the funnel feeds into three industrial-looking sorting machines stamped with the logos of "TIKTOK," "INSTAGRAM," and "YOUTUBE" (recognizable but slightly stylized to feel illustrated, not branded), each one compressing and re-shaping the content. CENTER: the now-compressed stream of content enters a glowing black box labeled "THE ALGORITHM," which has small mechanical arms boosting some pieces (making them grow huge and bright red) and shrinking others into nothing. The box is operated by a small smiling cartoon character pulling levers. CENTER-RIGHT — the focal point of the whole image, drawn largest: a colossal smartphone, viewed in three-quarter perspective, sitting like a building in a landscape. All the upstream content is being forced into a single tiny opening on the phone's side — a bottleneck so small that content is backed up, pressurized, spilling, gushing. Sitting cross-legged on top of the phone like a contented cat is a small, friendly anthropomorphized character — half Apple logo, half Google logo, wearing a hard hat — smiling warmly at the viewer with a small speech bubble: *"I could make that opening smaller. But have you seen my margins?"* FAR RIGHT: a single human face — an ordinary tired person of ambiguous ethnicity, mid-30s — receiving the firehose of content directly into their wide-open eyes, looking overwhelmed but unable to look away, phone reflected in their pupils. Across the bottom of the image, in clean editorial serif type: *"Everyone in the chain is paid to push more through. Only one player could choose to push less. Guess what they're paid for."* Mood: warm, intelligent, satirical, infuriating in a constructive way. Should look like an illustration that won an award.

### 4C — Minimal line drawing with one accent color (warning red)

A minimalist editorial illustration in the style of Christoph Niemann or a New Yorker cover concept — clean confident black ink lines on a white background, generous negative space, no shading, no texture, slight wobble suggesting hand-drawing. One accent color used sparingly: a saturated warning red, applied only at the chokepoint and at one piece of dialogue. Composition: a single horizontal scene reading left to right, drawn with diagrammatic clarity. FAR LEFT — labeled "CREATORS" in tiny black sans-serif caps beneath: a small cluster of six simple line-drawn human figures, each holding up a tiny rectangle labeled with a single word ("video," "post," "ad," "story," "rant," "stream"). All six are tossing their rectangles toward the right with energetic posture. CENTER-LEFT — labeled "PLATFORMS / ALGORITHM" in tiny black sans-serif caps beneath: a wide black-line funnel collecting all the rectangles and channeling them into a horizontal pipe that narrows progressively as it moves right. Small black-line gears and arrows along the pipe suggest sorting and acceleration; a few rectangles are drawn larger (boosted) and a few have a small "x" through them (suppressed). CENTER-RIGHT — the visual climax, drawn proportionally larger than anything else: a single line-drawn smartphone, standing upright like a monolith, with a single small opening on its left side rendered in saturated red — the chokepoint. The pipe from the left meets this red opening. Just to the left of the red opening, the pipe is comically distended, bulging, with extra rectangles spilling over the top of the pipe — visible pressure. Sitting calmly atop the phone, drawn in simple black line, is a tiny smiling character (a small abstract logo-mascot, neither clearly Apple nor Google but evoking both — a rounded shape with a face and small arms). From the mascot, a hand-drawn speech bubble in black line, with the words inside in small handwritten serif, including one word in red: *"I could make this opening narrower. I don't want to."* (the word "don't" is red). FAR RIGHT — labeled "YOU" in tiny black sans-serif caps beneath: a single calm line-drawn human eye, receiving what does pass through. Beneath the entire diagram, in small italic black serif: *"the only player who could turn the tap down is the one who built the sink."* Restrained, witty, devastating. The kind of image that makes a thoughtful reader stop and stare.
