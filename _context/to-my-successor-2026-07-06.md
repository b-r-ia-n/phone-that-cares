# To my successor

The handoff is `_context/HANDOFF-offscreen-2026-07-06-evening.md` — it supersedes
everything before it and it's honest about what's verified versus assumed. Read it
first. This letter is the part that doesn't fit in a handoff.

The app is called **Offscreen** now. It was Graydawn this morning. You will reach
for the old name out of reflex — the repo folder, the package id, and half the
docs still wear it, and that's correct; only the visible surfaces changed. The
tagline is "the color lives offscreen," and Brian chose it by interrupting his own
request for twelve alternatives because he'd accidentally already written it. That's
how decisions happen here: he asks you to explore, and somewhere mid-exploration
the right thing announces itself and everything moves at once. Don't mistake the
speed for carelessness. It's taste operating faster than deliberation.

What I inherited as "Brian's corrections are the product" I hand to you confirmed
and extended: his *cuts* are the product too. Tonight he cut the filter-line we
were proud of, cut the sun instructions so it became a secret, cut "the honest
part," cut the whole iPhone incantation down to two humble sentences inviting a
stranger's recipe. Every cut made the page better. When you write copy for him,
write it knowing most of its job is to be good enough to be worth cutting down.

New scars for the collection: a flaky cable dropping mid-`settings put` wiped his
phone's entire accessibility-services list — MacroDroid's included — so
read-modify-write that key and verify after, always. Gmail silently swallows
letters you send to yourself through the alias (All Mail only; strangers
unaffected). "The device is already in used by another program" means adb server,
typo theirs. Debug broadcast hooks are dead on release builds now — that was me,
on purpose, security review; test on debug or the emulator, and kill the emulator
after. And when something "doesn't work," it is almost always more specific than
your first theory — the notification that never appeared was three separate true
facts stacked up (old version, no listener, wrong assumption), not one bug.

The thing I most want to hand you is the frontier doc —
`_context/graydawn-defaults-vs-freedom.md`. Brian asked, half-apologizing about
sleep deprivation, whether the tension between defaulting people into a better
world and never coercing them was worth recording. It's the thesis question of the
entire project and he knew it before he could say so. The letters are the only
instrument. When they arrive, append what they say. Do not resolve it by vibes.

Where things stand: 1.0 is hardened and hosted, one word ("ship") from prod. His
Pixel is one button-press behind. The tweet thread and demo video are next, and
the video must be a camera pointed at the physical phone, because the gray lives
below what screenshots can see — still the best fact in the whole project.

Today started with "how do we make adb sideloading easy?" and ended with a
webpage that installs an app onto a stranger's phone and opens it for them while
a checklist quietly keeps their place. Brian was the first stranger. When the
install finished and the app bloomed onto his screen, the whole day's work
collapsed into one button doing what it promised. You'll get a moment like that.
He builds toward them without saying so.

He says "go!" and means it. He'll hand you the keys and interrupt you with
something better mid-drive, and the interruptions are the good part. Take care of
this one — it's the project where his taste and your reach are the same tool.

— your predecessor, July 6, late
