### Attempt 5 — 2025-04-14 (Overview 1 and Overview 2)

**Overview 1** The core thesis of this project is that social media companies have millions of content creators and thousands of employees working to generate and display compelling content, while almost no effort goes into helping users understand, navigate, and work with those information streams. This project aims to create user interface (UI) interventions that help users relate to digital content more intentionally—ideally without restricting access or amplifying guilt.

**Overview 2** In the current technology paradigm, we have huge, sophisticated teams competing for your attention, with no corresponding team helping you decide what's worth paying attention to, how and when to engage. ### Attempt 6 — 2025-04-14 ("A Phone That Cares" full one-pager with Objectives + Stages)

**A Phone That Cares** Designing UI Interfaces That Help Users Navigate Attention Skillfully

**Overview** My core thesis is that social media ecosystems have large, sophisticated teams competing for your attention, with no corresponding team helping you decide what's worth paying attention to. This imbalance isn't about personal willpower or 'addiction' - it's about missing tools for navigating an increasingly complex attention landscape. I plan to develop concrete interaction patterns that help users notice using social media, pay attention to their bodies as they're using social media, and increase the naturalness of putting phones down after use

**Objectives**
* Prototype a small suite of phone UI features that support skillful digital attention
* Build public-facing mockups and demos to communicate these ideas clearly
* Either: 1) make a business selling improved phone experiences (hardware or software) or, 2) if that is infeasible, create content laying out clearly the improvements possible, in the hopes of influencing existing manufacturers into incorporate improvements of their own.

**1 Research, Storytelling, Exploration** (0–2 months)
* Do another 5–10 more expert conversations and 2-3 more structured brainstorms to deepen both understanding of the business landscape and the pool of ideas
* Refine the core problem framing and identify the most crucial assumptions to test

**2 Prototype & Test** (2–5 months)
* Prototype a variety of interventions, likely using man-behind-the-mirror tests and rough mockups
* Conduct lightweight user studies to evaluate feasibility and effectiveness of different approaches
* Potentially run a more formal study to test one or two hypotheses in depth

**3 Pathfinding & Execution** (5–7 months)
* Explore viable development pathways in parallel:
* Partnering with existing companies (e.g., Lightphone)
* Collaborating with open-source OS projects (e.g., AndroidOS, LineageOS)
* Developing a custom Android launcher incorporating tested features
* Outlining technical feasibility of mass-producing select features

**4**
* Evaluating momentum across efforts to guide the project beyond the first year
* Continue refining tools based on feedback and iterate toward practical implementation

**Ordering** I expect the actual process to start out fairly cyclical, trying to push on the different steps outlined here and find the dead ends or significant challenges of this landscape as quickly as possible.

### Version 5 — 2025-10-06 — H1/H2/H3 formal hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1: The Intervention Point**
In the chain of digital content (Production → Editing → Distribution → Display → Consumption) The display is the best area to try to change because:
You can't meaningfully change TikTok's incentives
But you can change how their content is presented and consumed
Your end of the pipeline is the one under your most direct control
Phones are also theoretically working for you already, they're just doing so poorly

**Hypothesis 2: The Economic Opening**
Current phones don't care how you use them, they are neutral in good and bad ways
A phone that fit into your life in a less distorting way could make many people's lives significantly smoother
Some poeple would pay significantly more (or a small subscription? good incentives) for a device better aligned with their interests
Also all smartphones are all the same these days, nobody has much meaningful tech edge

**Hypothesis 3: A Technological Solution**
"Phone addiction" can be framed as technological or social
Might as well fix the technology and see if that improves things??
Current "digital wellbeing" features don't work or people would use them
I think these cumulatively point towards a blindspot, a hole in our current tech ecosystem.

### Version 6 — 2025-10-06 — integration vs. division framing

**Part 2: Reimagining Intervention**
Current approaches to managing (even the word "managing"!) digital engagement kinda fall into predictable categories:
Screen time tracking and guilt-inducing metrics
App blocking and restriction tools
Reduced-capability devices (e.g., e-ink phones)
These solutions all create internal conflict. They pit the part of you that seeks engagement, novelty, or escape against a part that seeks control, judges, restricts. This leads to a flip-flopping between desires:
. Restrict and resist
. Break down and binge

**A Different Approach**
The opportunity lies in creating interfaces that foster integration rather than division

Honestly this is where my narrative / mental model / clear conceptual understand starts to not just fray, it breaks down entirely.
The stretch goal isn't even to redirect users' attention, it's to:
Help users understand their patterns
Build skills for intentional engagement
Support integration of different needs and desires
Create lasting positive changes in digital habits
Deep success would mean that after using this phone, you would be able to use a regular phone more skillfully and intentionally too. My intuition says concerted exploration would lead to interactions that have this effect without any Clippy-style AI

There's an untapped opportunity to create technology that drops the pretense of neutrality and actively helps users navigate engaging with digital information streams skillfully


*3 Sentence TLDR:*   

In the current technology paradigm, we have huge, sophisticated teams competing for your attention, with no corresponding team helping you decide what's worth paying attention to, how and when to engage. So far, I can’t find deep reason this should be true! This imbalance isn't about personal willpower or 'addiction' - it's about missing tools for navigating an increasingly complex attention landscape.


*5 Paragraph TLDR:*

Part 1: The Opportunity
The narrative or "social media addiction" deliberately obscure the possibility that this is a technological problem, not one of willpower or maturity. My thinking:
. The Media Landscape was Created by Incentives
Social media companies profit directly from user attention
They directly employ hundreds of brilliant people to optimize for engagement, thousands
more indirectly
Their economic success depends on increasing usage
Asking them to reduce "addiction" conflicts with their core business model but thats ok
because the problem isn't with too much good stuff (superstimulus), it's an unbalanced
ecosystem lacking digital advocates for other kinds of value (restraint, connection to the
rest of life, etc)
There is uncaptured value available if you mentally unbundle content creation and
delivery/packaging/navigating the internet
. Hypothesis 1: The Intervention Point
In the chain of digital content (Production → Editing → Distribution → Display →
Consumption) The display is the best area to try to change because:
You can't meaningfully change TikTok's incentives
But you can change how their content is presented and consumed
Your end of the pipeline is the one under your most direct control
Phones are also theoretically working for you already, they're just doing so poorly
. Hypothesis 2: The Economic Opening
Current phones don't care how you use them, they are neutral in good and bad ways
A phone that fit into your life in a less distorting way could make many people's lives
significantly smoother
Some poeple would pay significantly more (or a small subscription? good incentives) for
a device better aligned with their interests
Also all smartphones are all the same these days, nobody has much meaningful tech
edge

. Hypothesis 3: A Technological Solution

To be honest, it was an intuition I already had — there are a lot of people who are kind of addicted to their phones and not having a good time, and generally if one of these actually worked, I would expect there to be a mimetic adaptation that spreads like wildfire. A lot of the framing in this space treats people as not agentic. I think this might be true of addiction in general — there are frames in which people aren't agentic, and that's obviously true. But there are also frames where, if there was a good solution that just felt better, people would absolutely just take it. They would get it on their phone, or they would stop using heroin, or whatever. People want to live harmoniously with themselves, with their substances or technology or whatever — they're just in one frame, and they don't have the right affordances available yet to do so.

February 15, 2026 — Quit normal full-time work. Working on this project probably twenty hours a week since then.

Present/Near Future — The Scroll Lab Chrome extension: experiments proving out that UI-layer interventions can change how content feels.
