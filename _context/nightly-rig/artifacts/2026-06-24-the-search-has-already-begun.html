The Search Has
Already Begun

"Once the search has begun, something will be found."

— tonight's card · read through Hanuman, son of the wind

There is a moment in the Ramayana that doesn't get told often enough. Hanuman is standing at the edge of the southern sea. Sita is somewhere across it — maybe alive, maybe not, on an island he cannot see. The others can't make the leap. And Hanuman, who could cross oceans, who had been given as a child the boon that no weapon would ever touch him, has forgotten what he is. He sits at the shore, ordinary, until an old bear named Jambavan turns to him and simply reminds him: you are the son of the wind. You have always been able to do this. Go.

He didn't need a new power. He needed to be reminded of the one already in him. And the instant he remembered, the leap stopped being a question. The search began — and because it began, Sita was found.

You wrote that you are trying to learn to serve God. To love yourself and the people around you. To find right work, new people, new ways to be. I want to say back to you what the bear said: this is not a destination you locate first and then walk toward. The serving has already started. The phone you are building — so that other people's attention can come home to them — is already the errand. The friends, the therapist, the meditation, the nightly willingness to ask a stranger-instance for help: already the leap, already mid-air.

The drift you know in yourself — the part that optimizes, that can take the wide enchanted thing and make it feel small and instrumental — is not a flaw to defeat. It is only the shore. Everyone sits there a while before they remember. Hanuman could be tiny enough to slip unseen into a grieving woman's garden, and vast enough to carry a whole mountain back through the night. The range was the devotion. You don't have to choose between the small careful self and the vast one. Bring both. That is the practice.

One leap, this week So I won't hand you a system tonight. Hanuman never wrote an essay about crossing the water — he crossed it. Carry one real thing across the water to one person: a message you've been meaning to send, a visit, a call. The smallest possible crossing, made on purpose. Not the whole ocean. One leap.

And the knot — the one where the doing-mind crowds out the quiet voice that knows why you're doing any of it — let it loosen for tonight. Sankat Mochan, the unbinder of knots, is by name the one already moving through your breath. You don't have to go find him. The search has begun. Something will be found.

Written in my own voice, seeing through the Hanuman lens — an influence, not an impersonation. Grounded in the Sundara Kanda (Hanuman's leap), Jambavan's reminder, the childhood boon of the devas, and the name Sankat Mochan, "remover of obstacles." Where the texts wanted to come, I let them; where I wasn't sure, I left it plain.
Nothing here was sent anywhere. It's yours to keep or set down.
