From h8r to superhuman guardian angel

This is part of a series of essays attempting to make a case that current smartphones are sorta outdated, that there is a surprising amount of low-hanging fruit, and to point towards that fruit. 

I want to give you a felt sense of possibility. I feel like current smartphones all look and behave about the same in the big picture, and I want to gesture at the space of *possibility* that makes this picture clear, right now in terms of your phone's relationship with you, the user. The scale below mixes together improvements along a few different axes of caring like capability, depth of understanding, and incentive alignment, to paint a more satisfying story of what a phone caring about you might look like, even if it's...

**Level 1: Active Harm.** Happily, most phones don't take this frame on too often. You can hate tiktok, but you won't buy a phone you hate. It might seem out of touch to some, but [phone makers](https://people.com/apple-ceo-tim-cook-urges-users-to-get-off-phones-and-experience-nature-11928105) don't tend to see their products as harmful. There are some exceptions, like tracking your behavior and selling it, or serving you ads you don't want but might go for anyways. I don't know whether phone data contributes to problem gamblers getting more DraftKings ads, but I wouldn't be surprised. On the whole though, I think most problems phones create in people's lives come from them being...

**Level 2: Indifferent.** The people who make and sell phones often think of them as neutral at worst. A knife company isn't at fault if used in a stabbing, why should your phone be blamed for the hours you spend scrolling? It's simply *serving you the content you asked for*. There are strong currents in technology culture framing information technology as *enabling* but not *altering* behavior, or of separating the *platform* speech happens on from the *content*. Some of these are necessary if you want to have a digital public space, but not all layers of technology seem like they necessarily *need* impartiality, especially if it mirrored your own. **Level 3: Helpful on Request.** Your current phone will help you with stuff, *if you ask*. And passive helpfulness is great! Opening google maps, setting a timer, looking stuff up are all direct affordances your phone offers. It also offers a version of this on the meta level - OS level stuff like deleting apps, setting screen time limits, organizing your home screen. Your phone will try and help you within the constraints its got, including low allowed agency. Some of these features get described as smart, but in practice feel more like a well-organized filing cabinet that occasionally talks. An opportunity here is initiative, that you most need a gentle intervention are the moments you're least likely to ask for one. This wouldn't be the problem if the phone acted like...

**Level 4: A Skilled Friend.** A phone at this level of capability would have some model of what you're interested in this week, not just what you're doing in a given moment. It might notice you've been spending a lot of time on an app but without enjoyment signals. And if it does, it doesn't block you, just makes something else a little more available. It can register the difference between focused work and anxious procrastination, and might even have the wisdom to let the scrolling let off steam for a bit before piping up. I believe the technology required to include some crude versions of this exist right now, even if we're definitely not yet at....

**Level 5: Angelic / Superhuman.** There's a concept in sci-fi of 'Angelic' superintelligences, those operating on a level that is effectively superhuman. Chess engines are a current example in this ability range, but technology at this level of *helpfulness* would be way more useful. A phone acting as guardian angel would hold a model of your flourishing more complete than anything you can hold yourself, it knows the difference between productive solitude and withdrawal, it knows just what to say to help unblock a stuckness. People who've spent time around certain meditators, exceptionally skilled managers or therapists sometimes describe something in this direction — the sense of being understood so thoroughly that large changes in the feeling of one's *world*, and in one's quality of life, can happen in a very short time.

Anyways, in there's four dimensions I think capture most of what I mean by "care" here:
-_depth_ (how richly a system understands you)
-_time horizon_ (whether it's thinking about your next 30 seconds or your next 30 years), 
-_incentive alignment_ (whether the system designers and manufacturers tend to only benefit when you do) and 
-*capability* (how good is it at helping you). 

You could write an essay about any of these, but for this essay I've tried to squish them all into one axis, imagining systems ranging from generally not-helpful to very. I have lots of particular ideas about how current systems might be more helpful, which I expect to post about shortly.
