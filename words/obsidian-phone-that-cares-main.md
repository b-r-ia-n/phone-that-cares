Notes:

### **⚙️ android codebase scale**

- full **AOSP** (android open source project) is _massive_: roughly **50–80 GB** of source code, ~**20 million+ lines**.
    
- what actually lives _on a phone_ after building: about **8–12 GB** once compiled (system image, apps, etc.).
    
- your macbook pro can totally handle it (just free up like **150 GB total** for build env + caches).
    
- most heavy stuff (compiling kernels, building images) happens locally via repo + make, but you’ll also pull dependencies and prebuilts from google’s servers.
    

---

### **💻 typical dev environment**

- most system stuff is **Java** and **Kotlin** (for framework / apps), **C/C++** for low-level parts, and **bash/python** for build tools.
    
- you’d mainly touch **Java/Kotlin**, maybe a bit of **C++** if you’re tweaking rendering or system compositor.
    
- most devs work in **VS Code**, **Android Studio**, or just **vim + tmux** if they’re deep kernel folks. android studio can handle AOSP builds but it’s heavy.
    

---

### **🤖 llm workflow**

  

best workflow rn:

- clone your fork to **GitHub** (public or private).
    
- use **GitHub Copilot**, **Claude Code**, or **Cursor IDE** (cursor.sh) — cursor’s good for navigating huge repos w/ llm context.
    
- you’ll mostly keep the code local, but these tools index it locally and talk to an llm through an API key — so no need to upload everything online.
    

---

### **📘 key resources**

1. **GrapheneOS Developer Docs** – solid entry to how a security-minded AOSP fork structures things.
    
2. **AOSP Build System Overview** (source.android.com/docs/setup) – explains how android actually compiles.
    
    bonus: the **LineageOS porting guide** is great for learning how community ROMs adapt base android to hardware.

phone that ~cares about you 

Part 1: The Opportunity 

The narrative or "social media addiction" deliberately obscure the possibility that this is a technological problem, not one of willpower or maturity. My thinking: 

. The Media Landscape was Created by Incentives 

Social media companies profit directly from user attention 

They directly employ hundreds of brilliant people to optimize for engagement, thousands more indirectly 

Their economic success depends on increasing usage 

Asking them to reduce "addiction" conflicts with their core business model but thats ok because the problem isn't with too much good stuff (superstimulus), it's an unbalanced ecosystem lacking digital advocates for other kinds of value (restraint, connection to the rest of life, etc) 

There is uncaptured value available if you mentally unbundle content creation and delivery/packaging/navigating the internet 

. Hypothesis 1: The Intervention Point 

In the chain of digital content (Production → Editing → Distribution → Display → Consumption) The display is the best area to try to change because: 

You can't meaningfully change TikTok's incentives 

But you can change how their content is presented and consumed 

Your end of the pipeline is the one under your most direct control 

Phones are also theoretically working for you already, they're just doing so poorly . Hypothesis 2: The Economic Opening 

Current phones don't care how you use them, they are neutral in good and bad ways A phone that fit into your life in a less distorting way could make many people's lives significantly smoother 

Some poeple would pay significantly more (or a small subscription? good incentives) for a device better aligned with their interests 

Also all smartphones are all the same these days, nobody has much meaningful tech edge

. Hypothesis 3: A Technological Solution 

"Phone addiction" can be framed as technological or social 

Might as well fix the technology and see if that improves things?? 

Current "digital wellbeing" features don't work or people would use them I think these cumulatively point towards a blindspot, a hole in our current tech ecosystem. Part 2: Reimagining Intervention 

Current approaches to managing (even the word "managing"!) digital engagement kinda fall into predictable categories: 

Screen time tracking and guilt-inducing metrics 

App blocking and restriction tools 

Reduced-capability devices (e.g., e-ink phones) 

These solutions all create internal conflict. They pit the part of you that seeks engagement, novelty, or escape against a part that seeks control, judges, restricts. This leads to a flip flopping between desires: 

. Restrict and resist 

. Break down and binge 

A Different Approach 

The opportunity lies in creating interfaces that foster integration rather than division Examples might include: 

Every time you open tiktok / instagram, the screen gradually drains of saturation over the course of an hour 

Half-screen modes, transparent modes (twitter-with-the-camera-on where tweets are overlaid on whatevers's behind your phone), content in literal picture frames to make it less of a portal 

Custom consumption patterns (e.g., one tweet at a time, tweets grouped into 'chapters' to give natural endpoints) 

Haptic / vibratory interaction to loop attention back towards body 

Interface layers that help you relate to content differently, like alternate Bluesky algorithm 

Not all of these even make sense as a phone! There could be desktop or mobile content aggregator + display applications, media consumption specific devices, i have no idea!

There's a whole layer of technology that it feels like it missing to me - stuff that works for you, and that's what i want to point towards / flesh out. 

Ok butlike how's thatrelate to integration or whatever 

Honestly this is where my narrative / mental model / clear conceptual understand starts to not just fray, it breaks down entirely. 

The stretch goal isn't even to redirect users' attention, it's to: 

Help users understand their patterns 

Build skills for intentional engagement 

Support integration of different needs and desires 

Create lasting positive changes in digital habits 

Deep success would mean that after using this phone, you would be able to use a regular phone more skillfully and intentionally too. My intuition says concerted exploration would lead to interactions that have this effect without any Clippy-style AI bot being needed.
