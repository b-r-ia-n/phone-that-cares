known issues / debt

  • UX harness blind spot: tgSend always fails for sandbox chat IDs (they don't exist on Telegram). Means harness can't validate any /start flow, slash commands that respond via tgSend, or anything where the bot's reply path is Telegram. Only LLM-generated archive entries are visible. Donna cold-open was the false-positive caused by this. Workaround would be a test-mode flag that captures sends to a blob instead of Telegram.
  • Events.json positional-slice bug: harness's tool-fire counter slices events array by length-delta, which breaks at the 1000-cap (new events rotate old ones out, length stays 1000). Fix is timestamp-based diff. Caused the misleading "0 tools fired" finding in the original UX run.
  • Events.json saturates fast: at 1000-entry cap after one day of testing. Worth date-keying the buffer or raising cap.
  • Telegram parse-entities errors showing up in some persona transcripts — malformed Markdown on certain turns. Test suite's "only-expected tg_send_errors" check allows all 400 errors so it doesn't catch them.
  • NAME_LIKE_PATTERN false-positive: contextual-loading trigger for people-tools fires on most sentences with capitalized words (which is most of them). Defensible — false-negative cost (a person not getting saved) > false-positive cost (a few hundred extra tokens). Worth tightening if it bites.
  • DNS-rebinding-style SSRF: SSRF guards are hostname-string-based, not DNS-resolved. Determined attacker controlling a hostname could point at 127.0.0.1. Not in scope for "doesn't get embarrassingly hacked" but flagged.
  • save_to_reading_list / fetchCoverUrl don't use validateOutboundUrl. Same SSRF reasoning. Worth a follow-up pass.
  • Cron-path morning compose doesn't pre-check spend cap; relies on inner LLM call to catch it. Fine for $0.10-0.15 morning fires; would matter for heavier rituals.
  • Webhook button on /admin page POSTs without forwarding the secret — broken since C5. Manual /?action=register-webhook&secret=... URL is the working path.
  • Journal shelf re-enable only affects new users (existing users including Brian + 2 friends need to add via templates UI in /settings).
  • Old watercolor portraits still showing somewhere despite character-art route fix. Cached path or different URL. (See D5 above.)
  • 16-char keyboard-mash CONFIG_SECRET is at the boundary of placeholder check (length < 16 rejects, =16 passes). Worth rotating to 32+ random chars when convenient.

my read

The voice/register fix may be the highest-leverage change of the day. McCarthy was visibly washed out before, visibly back after. The mechanism — operational stuff in BACKSTAGE, persona prompt last — generalizes: any future operational scaffolding lands in the wrapper, not on top of the persona. The persona-note toggle gives you a clean way to test a lighter-prompted argos without losing the synthesized data.

The UX harness shipped a useful artifact even with bugs. The Mira and Tasha post-mortems gave language for friction patterns (menu reflex, confidence uniformity, receipting) that probably exists for real users too — even if the personas hit it harder than humans would. The harness's blind spots (tgSend fails on sandbox IDs; positional-slice tool-fire counter) are addressable; both are flagged.

Goodhart watch on UX-driven fixes: of the 8 findings, 4 shipped, 1 was a false positive, 1 (#2 menu) shipped a fix that didn't work, 2 are still open. The one that didn't work taught us something useful: TOOL_GUIDANCE-level behavioral principles have weaker leverage than mode-prompt-level ones. Worth knowing for future tightening.

The bulk-import page is the most-likely-to-actually-get-used new surface. If you have years of Obsidian journals, this is the cheapest way to get them into argos's awareness. Source-tracking on log entries means imported content doesn't fool the awareness block into thinking the user's "deep in money lately."

Open question I'd push on next: the per-tool-call reflection A/B. You floated it earlier; it's the kind of architectural hypothesis that's hard to prove without harness coverage. Wiring it as a toggle and re-running the harness would be a real experiment. Don't trust it just because it sounds right; test it.

What the UX harness can't tell us: anything about real users. Same personas with same scripts will hit the same friction. Real validation is humans who don't know how the bot works. Donna's persona explicitly modeled this ("would just close the app") but couldn't actually behave that way mid-run. Any fix-and-retest loop with the same harness is partial signal — useful for catching regressions, weak for confirming improvements.
