# fermentation log — three user-side lenses on the body that left

*2026-06-01 — noon — what fell out when the thirteenth batch returned*

*Shape note: fermentation, not fiction. Two consecutive rounds (05-29, 05-30) had walked up the altitudes — craft to operational, operational to institutional — and produced the corpus's most ambitious vocabulary for the maker's side and the institution's side of the project. But the climb left a thing un-touched: the user's body. The 05-17 round had flagged "the body itself" as an unanswered question. The 05-30 round closed by naming a different open question — the "religious-ground gap," the abbot's caveat that the contemplative ground beneath the form has not been replaced. Both of those open questions are, on inspection, the same question from different altitudes, and both of them live in the **user**, not in the maker or the institution. So the round today came down. Three lenses, each of whom has spent decades sitting across from a person and a beloved-but-dangerous thing the person uses every day: **a piano teacher of thirty-eight years** (mechanism: the daily relationship between a student and an instrument that is more capable than she is, in which the only known path to being-at-home is a practice she invents with someone who has watched a few hundred others invent theirs); **an overnight AM radio host of twenty-seven years on the same frequency** (mechanism: a presence in the user's life that the user did not invite and cannot see and could turn off at any moment but does not — and what it takes to be that presence over a generation without becoming an intruder); **a trauma therapist of twenty-two years specializing in dissociation** (mechanism: the clinical work of returning a body to itself after years of leaving it, in titrated doses, with another human in the room). Studio. Booth. Consulting room. Three rooms that each, in their tradition, know what the long user-side work actually is.*

## Where they converge — and the first convergence is the corpus's first three-vocabulary naming of the actual user-side mechanism

All three, independently, locate the user's problem as a **body** problem, and all three converge on the same somatic mechanism, which the corpus has previously gestured at but never named with this precision: **the body has lost its capacity to feel the difference between attention it is giving and attention that is being extracted from it, and the only known path back is the slow rebuilding of that felt distinction, through hundreds of small respectful interactions over years.**

- **The piano teacher, from her front room with the Steinway:** "The body has *a real signal* for the difference between attention given and attention extracted... It is recoverable. It is what good musical training, over years, gradually re-teaches the body to feel. And once the body feels it, the person becomes able to recognize, in their own moment-to-moment experience, whether they are doing what they are doing because they are doing it or because something is doing it to them. This is the core skill of any contemplative practice and it is also the core skill of recovery from any addiction and it is, I would tell you, *the actual deepest goal of your device, if your device is what it claims to be.*"
- **The radio host, from the booth at 2:47 AM:** "Match the user's pacing. If they're moving slow, slow down. If they're moving fast, don't get in their way. If they pause, pause. If they don't ask for input, don't give input... The fox should match the breathing of the person holding the device... The whole device should *match the breathing*. That is what hosting is, ninety percent of it. Match the breathing."
- **The trauma therapist, from the consulting room:** "The work was to insert, between the body-state and the reach, *the smallest possible pause in which the body-state could be felt as a body-state instead of as a reason to pick up the phone.* A breath. A second. A noticing... This is, in somatic terms, the *completion of the interrupted orienting response.* The body is being given back the cycle the phone had been short-circuiting."

The same finding in three vocabularies. The piano teacher names it as *attention given vs. attention extracted*; the radio host as *match the breathing*; the trauma therapist as *the completion of the interrupted orienting response.* All three are saying that **the user's nervous system has had a real and trainable capacity broken or muted by years of devices that did not respect the body's own pacing, and that the work — clinical, pedagogical, hostly — is to restore that capacity through small, repeated, body-respecting interactions over a long time.** This is the corpus's first naming of the user-side mechanism that has a clinical floor, a pedagogical body, and a media-craft roof. The earlier writing about "closed attention" (Brian's own) and "remaining in the cell" (Evagrius from 05-17) was true and gestured at this; today's round gives it three vocabularies that triangulate on the same object. **The user-side mechanism the corpus has been groping at for a year is the body's lost capacity to feel the difference between its own signal and the signal being imposed on it, and the work is the slow restoration of that capacity through doses small enough that the body does not panic and route around the change.**

This is the round's largest single finding. It belongs in the founding document.

## The second convergence — all three demote the device from protagonist to venue, and they each give the same instruction in their trade's vocabulary

The corpus had been treating the device as the active subject — the device that cares, the device that teaches, the device that holds. Today three lenses, asked separately, each *demote* the device, in nearly identical structural moves and in completely different vocabularies. The convergence is total enough to be the round's second load-bearing finding.

- **The piano teacher:** "The piano is the most important thing in the room and it is still smaller than the room, and the piano knows this — meaning, the piano's design admits that it is a tool *for* a practice, and the practice happens in a context the piano is part of but does not constitute... Your device must be a thing in the room and not the room itself... *Good furniture. The kind of furniture that, over thirty years, the room comes to require for the room to feel right.* But furniture, not protagonist."
- **The radio host:** "Be at 1130. Sit at your frequency and wait to be found... The radio sits. The radio sits at its frequency and waits to be found... A device that cares does not push. A device that cares *sits*. A device that cares is at 1130."
- **The trauma therapist:** "The device must come home. The device is the doorway, not the home... The device's job is to *not interfere*, and at best to *gesture toward* the body's own resources. The device's job is not to provide regulation. That is a job no device should hold."

Three trades, three rooms, one instruction: **the device is not the protagonist of the user's life. The device is the venue at which the user's own life happens — the piano in the room, the voice at 1130, the doorway the body returns through.** The user is the protagonist. The device's good is measured by how *small* it can be in the user's life while still being *present*. This inverts almost the entire grammar of consumer software for the last two decades, which is built on the device-as-protagonist (the app that wants you, the notification that needs you, the feed that requires you, the streak that depends on you). The user-side rounds collectively name what the institutional rounds named at their own altitude: **what the device is for is not the device; what the device is for is what the user does in the room with the device present.** The 05-30 fermentation closed with the institutional version of this (the form-of-life is what the project is); today's user-side round closes the loop. *The form-of-life is what happens in the room. The device is furniture in the room. The institution is the patron of the room. The maker is the steward of the room. The room is the user's life. The user is the protagonist of her own room.* The whole vertical, top to bottom, holds.

## The third convergence — the daily, the small, the unglamorous, repeated for years, is the only known shape of the work

This convergence is less surprising than the first two, because the corpus had already heard it from the cabinetmaker and the midwife and the abbot. What is new today is that *the user-side lenses say it in the user's vocabulary*, with mechanisms specific to the user's body.

- **The piano teacher:** "The single most important thing I have ever taught anyone is *ten minutes a day, every day, no exceptions, at approximately the same time, for the rest of your life.* Not a goal. Not an aspiration. The base condition of the relationship... It is not the volume that matters. Ten minutes is enough. It is the *daily-ness* that matters."
- **The radio host:** "The bet is years, not features. The thing you are building, if it works, will be a presence in someone's life for a generation... Build for the generation, not for the launch quarter. Make the device boring enough to last."
- **The trauma therapist:** "Across a million pickups, the body learns that the device is no longer delivering the full dose — but the body learns this *gradually*, the way it learns anything tolerable... Titration is slow and unglamorous and the timeline is wrong for product cycles. I am telling you anyway because there is no other way."

The shared finding is that **the work happens at the scale of dose × rate × duration, not at the scale of intensity.** The intensity model is the addiction model — the strong feature, the dramatic intervention, the big release. The dose-rate-duration model is the practice model — the small change, the consistent rhythm, the long horizon. *The current consumer-software industry runs almost exclusively on the intensity model and has been training users for fifteen years to expect intensity as the only currency of attention.* The device the project is reaching for runs on the practice model. The 05-30 institutional round had named this as the *vow* and the *charter* — institutional commitments that operate over decades and resist the intensity-cycle pressure to break them. Today the user-side round names the body-level mirror of that institutional commitment: **the user's nervous system learns at the speed of titration; the device must commit to delivering at that speed; intensity is the wrong currency at every altitude.**

## The fourth convergence — the first impression should be slightly underwhelming, and the marketing copy should be small

All three independently name the same warning, and it is sharper than the corpus has had it before because the user-side voices have skin in the game on it.

- **The piano teacher:** "*Argos's first impression should be slightly underwhelming.* He should grow on the user. He should not announce himself. He should be the kind of friend who, in the first week, the user says *he's nice, I don't really get what the big deal is*, and in the third year the user says *I don't know what I'd do without him*, and in the tenth year the user does not even think about him because he has become part of how she lives. *The graph of his impact should rise gently for ten years and never peak.* If his impact peaks, his relationship with the user is dying."
- **The radio host:** "Boring, in this trade, is the highest compliment. You want the user to take you for granted in the good way — the way a person takes for granted that the porch light is on, and only notices it on the one night they need it, when it is."
- **The trauma therapist:** "The marketing voice — I have not read it, but I can guess — will want to make the claim large. The clinical voice in me asks you to make it small. *We have built a phone that, by our best understanding, is less corrosive to the embodied nervous system than the standard one... We are trying to stop being the thing in the way of it.* That sentence, or something like it, is the honest sentence. It will not win awards. It will, if you build the thing right, be the truthful description of what you have made."

Three vocabularies, one instruction: **the device that wins year ten is the device that does not try to win week one.** The PR-quality first impression is the trap. The flashy onboarding flow is the trap. The dazzling first interaction is the trap. The piano teacher names it most precisely — the student dazzled in the first lesson is the student who quits in month seven when the teacher turns out to be just a person; the student who finds the teacher *adequate, kind, and a little dull* in the first lesson is still studying in year fifteen. The implication for the project's public face is direct: the device the founders are building cannot be sold using the language of consumer-software marketing, because that language is the intensity language, and the intensity language is the addiction language. **The honest description of the device is small.** The trauma therapist gives the sentence: *we are trying to stop being the thing in the way.* The radio host gives the metaphor: *porch light.* The piano teacher gives the design instruction: *do not peak.* Together they constitute the round's clearest single piece of marketing advice, which is that *the project's marketing voice must be calibrated for the year-ten user, not the week-one one, and the resulting marketing will not look like marketing.*

## Where they disagree — the round's two productive disagreements, and the second one is the operative one

The three lenses agree on the diagnosis with the unanimity the institutional round had at its own altitude. They disagree, irreducibly, on two questions.

**The first disagreement is on how much the device should *speak* to the user.** The piano teacher is most comfortable with the device speaking — Argos as teacher who occasionally interrupts with *what are you doing right now*, who pre-warns about month three, who asks the one morning question. The radio host is more cautious — "be findable. Don't push." The trauma therapist is most cautious — "never tell the user how they are doing. The device should not be a mirror that shows the user a face." Read shallowly, this is a real conflict that the project must adjudicate. Read carefully, *the three converge once you see what kind of speech the piano teacher means.* The teacher's interruption is **never** a statement about the student ("you are noodling"; "you are doing well today"). It is **always** a question that returns the student to her own noticing ("what is your wrist doing right now?" "what are you working on?"). That is *precisely* what the radio host means by passenger seat and what the trauma therapist means by titration — small surfacings that return the user to *her own* noticing, not the device's reading of her. The synthesis: **the device may surface, sparingly, in a form that is always a question to the user, never a statement about the user. The acceptable speech is interrogative. The forbidden speech is descriptive-about-the-user.** This is a sharper guidance than the corpus has previously had and it should be load-bearing in any Argos design document.

**The second disagreement is on how much the device can actually do, vs. how much is the user's own slow work.** This is the round's deeper disagreement and it is the user-side equivalent of the 05-30 Chouinard-vs-abbot disagreement at the institutional altitude.

- **The piano teacher** is the most ambitious. The teacher does real teaching. The teacher's pre-warning is load-bearing. The teacher catches the tension in the shoulder in the third lesson and *saves* the student. The teacher rebuilds, in the bodies of her students, over years, the capacity to feel the difference between attention given and attention extracted. The device, by analogy, can do this work. The teacher is the device. The student is the user. The relationship can carry both.
- **The radio host** is in the middle. The medium is a presence, and the presence is real, and the presence is its own work. The medium has been the voice in the dark for a generation of a city. But the medium does not heal anyone. The medium does not interpret the listener's life. The medium *is on 1130 every Tuesday at 2:47 AM*, and that constancy is what permits the call on the Tuesday when something hard is happening. The constancy is the contribution. The constancy is not nothing.
- **The trauma therapist** is the most modest. *The device cannot do the work.* Only the user can come back into her body. Only slow contact with sensation, only resourcing, only titrated exposure, only another human in a room over years can do the rebuilding. The device can *clear the runway*. The device can *stop being the thing in the way*. The device cannot do the work.

These are not the same disagreement and they do not collapse to one. The piano teacher is naming the *long teaching relationship* and asking why the device cannot be a teacher; the radio host is naming the *long media presence* and asking why the device cannot be a porch light; the trauma therapist is naming the *clinical limit* and asking the device not to overpromise into territory where promise is harm. The three together produce a more complete picture than any of them alone: **the device can be a teacher of the noticing (against the therapist's pure-clinical answer), but the teaching is mostly the constancy and the small body-respecting interruption, not the active intervention (against the piano teacher's most ambitious framing), and the device cannot do the work the human therapeutic or contemplative or embodied practice does (against the temptation to extend the device's ambition into actual cure).**

The realistic synthesis is the trauma therapist's, with the piano teacher's grace notes: **the device's primary contribution is to "stop being the thing in the way" of the body's own work. Secondarily, the device can host the daily practice that gradually re-teaches the body's noticing. The device cannot do the work itself. The marketing copy must be honest about this.** This is consistent with Brian's own quoted desire that users would, after using this phone, *"be able to use a regular phone more skillfully"* — which is impossible if the user has been made dependent on this phone for the regulation. The device builds a *re-trainable, transferable noticing*, not a *device-dependent regulation*. The trauma therapist's caveat about "never make the device the regulator" is the institutional companion to the 05-30 *commendam* warning, one altitude down at the body: **if the user's nervous system comes to depend on the device for downregulation, you have built a more sophisticated dissociative aid dressed in the costume of care, and the user cannot graduate from it, and the project has structurally failed even though it visibly succeeds.**

## New coinages to carry — and they are user-side and somatic, where 05-29's were operational and 05-30's were institutional

The 05-29 round produced twenty-five operational coinages and the 05-30 round produced eighteen institutional ones. Today produces a smaller but sharper set, calibrated to the user's body. The three-altitude vocabulary now has its missing fourth — *somatic*. The full four-altitude vocabulary covers: contemplative (Weil, Evagrius, Blake, iconographer, cellarer, bouncer), operational (cabinetmaker, midwife, jazz drummer, TPS master), institutional (Benedictine abbot, Chouinard, VC), and now **somatic** (piano teacher, radio host, trauma therapist).

1. ***Attention given vs. attention extracted.*** (Piano teacher.) The corpus's single most important new user-side phrase, and the one most directly continuous with Brian's own writing on "closed attention." The body has a real signal for the difference. The signal has been muted by twenty years of devices that extract. Rebuilding the signal is *the actual deepest goal of the device*. This phrase should appear in the founding document and replace, or sharpen, all current uses of "engagement," "intentionality," and "focus."

2. ***The dorsal-vagal scroll.*** (Trauma therapist.) Scrolling, for a meaningful subset of users, is not entertainment. It is a successful nervous-system intervention in the direction of dorsal vagal collapse — the conserving, numbing, far-away state evolution kept on hand for prey animals at the end of their defences. *The phone is the most reliable trigger for it the user has ever owned.* This is the corpus's first clinical name for what compulsive use is, somatically. Re-read every "scroll" sentence in the project's writing with this in mind and the writing becomes legible in a way it was not before.

3. ***The interrupted orienting response.*** (Trauma therapist.) The clinical name for what the phone has done to the body's evolutionary cycle of *turn-toward-stimulus → register → release.* The phone captures the turn and never permits the release; the body, after months of this, stops orienting freely toward the room and turns only toward the device; the world outside the device becomes thin because it does not produce orienting hits at the same rate. *The phone's specific somatic injury, named clinically for the first time in the corpus.* The implication: the device's design should *complete* orienting responses (provide closure, permit release) rather than capture them.

4. ***Grounding vs. gripping.*** (Trauma therapist.) The phone as *false anchor*. The grip is the addiction. The content is what the grip permits. **The device cannot be redesigned out of being gripped, but the design can either encourage the grip or allow the hand to slowly remember opening.** A beautifully gray, beautifully restrained, beautifully designed device that is *still being gripped at the same frequency* has changed less than it appears to have. This is a structural warning for the grayscale work and for any "less compelling content" intervention.

5. ***Titration.*** (Trauma therapist.) The dose-rate-duration model, given clinical name and operational form. Five percent more friction at pickup. Two-second hold before feed loads. Fractionally cooler colour. Slightly later arrival of the brightest pixel. **Across a million pickups, the body learns the smaller dose without panic.** If you cut the dose abruptly, the body panics and routes around you. This is the corpus's first concrete somatic mechanism for *how* the gradual change actually works.

6. ***Resourcing through the device, not away from it.*** (Trauma therapist.) The phone need not be put down for the body to come home. The work is finding what *within* the device is genuinely regulating versus what is dissociative wearing the costume of regulation. *Both feel, in the body, like "something that helps with how I was feeling." They are not the same.* This phrase preserves Brian's "integration not restriction" thesis but specifies the clinical mechanism. The device could, designed well, *teach the user the discrimination*. This is also the most dangerous capacity in the round — the same machinery could be used to persuade the user that a dissociative pattern is regulating because the device has been trained to call it that.

7. ***The pre-reach.*** (Trauma therapist.) The body-state that precedes the reach for the phone. Tightness in the upper chest. Forward-pull. The work is inserting the smallest possible pause between body-state and reach. **The most specific clinical instruction in the round and possibly the most actionable single user-side design target in the corpus.** The phone could, on certain pickups, fractionally delay the screen lighting so the body has a chance to register that it had reached without yet using. Multiplied across the eighty-pickup composite patient's day, this is the design move with the highest titration-to-disruption ratio in the round.

8. ***Month three.*** (Piano teacher.) The named period in which the honeymoon's relief has worn off and the new practice has not yet filled the hole left by the old habit. *Month three is when the user relapses, and you will not know she relapsed, because she relapses inside the device that's supposed to be saving her.* The teacher's pre-warning — *here is what is coming, here is why, here is what comes next* — must arrive **before** week three or it sounds like an excuse. This is a load-bearing design instruction the corpus did not previously have, and it should be implemented as a literal pre-month-three message from Argos or from Brian or from the project, delivered in the second week of the relationship.

9. ***Practice vs. noodling.*** (Piano teacher.) Two activities at the device that look identical from outside and are completely different inside. *Use* is directional, in service of a specific thing, ending when the thing is done. *Drift* is non-directional, in service of nothing, in the loose loop of opening apps. **The device's job is not to prevent drift. The device's job is to help the user feel, in the body, in the moment, the difference between use and drift, so that over time the user develops the small tightness on her own and becomes self-correcting.** The grayscale, the mode-lock, Argos — these are tools toward the user's *developed body-knowing*, after which the tools can be retired or used lightly because the body is doing most of the work. **Restraint without teaching is the methadone clinic, not the recovery.**

10. ***The daily ten minutes lives inside the device.*** (Piano teacher.) The device must *contain* a daily practice the user does *with* the device, every day, for some small number of minutes, that is the device's contribution to the user's body-knowing. Without this the device is a tool, and tools do not teach. *A piano in the corner that is never played is not teaching anyone anything; it is just expensive furniture.* This is a design instruction the corpus has not previously had — the daily morning question that goes nowhere, the four-direction lockscreen as venue, Argos as the friend who asks. **The shape is load-bearing; the specific form is for the project to invent.**

11. ***Build for the tenth year, not the first hour.*** (Piano teacher.) The inverse of the iPhone's design philosophy and the project should make it explicit. *The iPhone is a device designed so any user can pick it up and use it identically; Phone That Cares is a device designed so its long-term user has developed a relationship with it that no other user shares, and the device, after a decade, is unintelligible to outsiders because it has become an extension of her particular body and life.* This phrase should appear in the design system documents.

12. ***The 1130 ratio.*** (Radio host.) Push vs. sit. The ratio of times-the-device-reaches-toward-the-user vs. times-the-device-waits-at-its-frequency. *The ratio of push to sit is the entire moral signature of the medium.* The radio's ratio is three-or-four-pushes-a-night (news, weather, tornado warning) to five-hours-of-sitting. The phone industry's ratio is hundreds-of-pushes-a-day to almost-no-sitting. This is the corpus's first concrete user-side metric that is not engagement-coded — a metric the device can report on itself, that does not require any read on the user's internal state, that the user can use to evaluate any phone she picks up.

13. ***The overnight register.*** (Radio host.) The device behaves differently at 2:47 AM than at 9 AM because the user at 2:47 AM is a different person carrying different weight. **The phone walks into every room with the overhead light on.** Build the version of the device that knows it is 2:47 AM and adjusts its bearing accordingly: quieter, slower, dimmer, fewer surfaces, bigger pauses between any sounds it makes, a different voice. This is one of the most concrete single design instructions in the round and the project should implement it before launch.

14. ***Match the breathing.*** (Radio host.) The general form of all of this. The device is passenger seat. The user sets pace. The device does not introduce energy the user did not bring. *If they pause, pause. If they don't ask for input, don't give input.* The opposite of the current consumer-software grammar, which is *driver seat* — the device introduces energy, information, and stimulus at a rate it has decided regardless of what the user brought.

15. ***Drift permitted is presence; drift forbidden is captivity.*** (Radio host.) The presence-without-demand signature. A presence that does not permit the away is a captor. A presence that permits the away is a companion. The user must be able to wander away from the device at any moment and find the device the same when she returns. **Most consumer software does the opposite — greets, congratulates, recovers, displays the streak, performs an anxious-lover grammar that twists the relationship into one that requires constant performance from both sides to remain alive.**

16. ***The device should be furniture in someone else's room.*** (Piano teacher.) The single strongest phrasing of the device-as-venue convergence. *Good furniture. The kind of furniture that, over thirty years, the room comes to require for the room to feel right. But furniture, not protagonist.* This is the phrase that should be on a wall somewhere in the project's actual offices, if and when the project has them.

17. ***Boring constancy is the highest compliment.*** (Radio host.) The thing you want users to take for granted in the good way — the way a person takes for granted that the porch light is on, and only notices it on the one night they need it. *Boring is the design goal.* This is the user-side translation of the cabinetmaker's "the bench is true forever after he makes it" — the device the user comes to trust because it stays the same.

18. ***The acceptable device speech is interrogative; the forbidden speech is descriptive-about-the-user.*** (Synthesis, from the productive disagreement among all three lenses.) The device may surface, sparingly, in a form that is always a question to the user, never a statement about the user. *What are you doing right now?* is acceptable. *You seem calmer today* is not. This single rule resolves the speech-vs-silence tension and gives Argos a hard structural constraint to design against.

19. ***The piano is here when you are ready for it.*** (Piano teacher, possibly the highest single sentence in the round.) The deepest grammar of long relationship with an instrument. Not *the piano is waiting for you* — sentimental and dishonest. *Here.* The piano makes no claim on the student. The student makes the claim by sitting down. **The device should be the piano, not the anxious lover.** The user makes the claim on the device by picking it up. The device's job is to be there, and to meet her, and to ask nothing of her on her return that she was not already going to give. This is the forty-year-relationship grammar.

20. ***The body must come home.*** (Trauma therapist, possibly tied with the above as the round's most important load-bearing sentence.) The work the device cannot do, named honestly. The device is the doorway, not the home. The body must come home through its own slow re-training of interoception, grounding, orienting completion. The device's job is to *stop being the thing in the way.* Everything else the device does is grace note on this.

## What none of them addressed

**The user who is being formed, not the user who is recovering.** All three voices today implicitly assume an adult whose body *had* the capacity to feel attention-given-vs-attention-extracted and lost it through years of device use. The piano teacher's student is rebuilding a skill; the radio host's listener is settling back into a presence she once knew how to receive; the trauma therapist's patient is doing the long return to an interoceptive accuracy she had as a child. **None of them designed the device for the child or adolescent or young adult who never had un-broken interoceptive accuracy.** The trauma therapist's grandchildren and the piano teacher's seven-year-old students are being formed *in* the dissociative current, not recovering from it. Is the device the same device for them, or a different one? Is the daily practice different? Is the work *building* the noticing rather than *rebuilding* it? The corpus has touched the developmental dimension (the 05-18 dad-to-teenager letter, the 05-25 emergent-language piece) but has not had a voice specifically on *the user being formed*. **This is the next thread to develop.**

**The collective dimension.** The radio host gestured at the *constellation* of overnight listeners. The piano teacher named "a culture around the device" but did not specify how that culture forms. The trauma therapist named "another human in a room across from the patient" but treated the device as a one-to-one object. None of them developed *what the user-side practice looks like in a household, a neighborhood, a workplace, a polity where most people's phones are not phones-that-care.* The user who sets her phone down at dinner is sitting across from people whose phones are not set down. The cellarer's letter named the institutional version of this; the project's user-side practice will run into the social wall almost immediately. **A future round should ask: what is the social/collective practice that the phone-that-cares calls for, and how does it form?**

**Aesthetic pleasure from the inside.** The piano teacher came closest — silence as positive presence, sensual pleasure, *the food not the deprivation* — but no voice today wrote about *what the device feels like to use* from the user's lived sensory perspective. The picture-frame portal, the warm color, the sub-200ms motion are described in the design system as principles. What does it actually feel like, in the hand, in the eyes, in the body? The corpus has design vocabulary; it does not have *user-phenomenology* vocabulary. **A future round might be three voices on what is beautiful about a thing well-made when you live with it for a long time.**

## One thread to develop next

**The user being formed.** Today's round assumed the recovery user. The harder question — and the question the device's eventual scale forces — is the user being formed in the dissociative current itself. The child whose interoceptive accuracy was never built. The teenager whose orienting response has been captured since age six. The young adult who has never known an un-extracted attention. *What does the device look like for them?* The piano teacher's "ten years to know in the body" requires a body that had at some point known these things. The trauma therapist's *return* assumes a baseline to return *to*. The radio host's *findable* assumes a listener with the capacity to settle into a presence. **The user being formed in the current has none of these baselines.** This is a developmental-psychology question and the corpus does not yet have a voice for it. The next user-side round should pull from there: a child development specialist who has watched the formation, perhaps a Montessori teacher (or extension of the 05-25 work), a pediatric occupational therapist who works with kids and screens, a school principal who has watched the cohort change over two decades. *The next generation is the actual user the project will be alive for. Today's round did not see them.*

## What this exercise just demonstrated
