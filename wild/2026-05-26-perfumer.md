*2026-05-26 — a perfumer, called for the user who feels no lack*

The Montessori voice told you how a capacity *grows* around a worthy object, in a still room, beside a teacher who withholds her hands. It assumed a child who would, given the choice, turn toward the cylinders rather than the bright thing. The fermentation log caught what that assumes and could not supply: the *wanting.* The captured adult turns toward the worthy object because some part of him remembers it and aches. The native user remembers nothing. She does not choose the worthy object over the bright one because she has no felt reason to — the feed sated her before a hunger could form. You can build the room, forge the rep, convene the unharvested square, and she will stand in the middle of all of it, unmoved, because none of it has *happened to her yet.*

I am a nose. I will tell you the one thing my craft knows that yours keeps trying to talk its way around: **you cannot make a person want a scent by describing it.** I have watched the best writers alive lay sentences in front of a deadened nose — *green, animalic, a wet stone after rain, the inside of an old book* — and the nose nods and feels nothing, because language is downstream of the wanting, not upstream. The word "lilac" has never once made anyone's chest open. Description addresses someone who *already has* the appetite and only needs directions. The native user does not need directions. She has no appetite. This is the precise shape of your gap, and it is the shape I face every day across the counter.

Here is the mechanism, stated so you can break it if it's wrong. **Desire for the real is not grown and not argued into being. It is lit by ambush — by the actual, irreplaceable thing put under the nose once, unbidden, before the mind can raise its guard.** Real attar of rose is not a strong smell. It is a *specific* one: it has a green knife in it and a rot under the sweetness and a third thing no one can name, and when it lands on a nose that has only ever known the rose of shampoo and air-freshener, the nose does not think *that is nice.* It flinches. Something in the body registers, before any opinion forms, *I did not know this existed and now I have lost something I never had.* That flinch is the ignition. Not pleasure — pleasure the synthetic supplies in floods. The flinch is closer to a small wound: beauty enters from the front, under the guard, and leaves an absence shaped like itself. **The wanting is the shape of that wound.** You do not want what you have never been pierced by. You cannot be pierced by a description. You can only be pierced by the actual.

Now the dark twin, because it is your native user exactly. My craft has a counterfeit: synthetic fragrance engineered to *mimic, flood, and deaden.* It is louder than the real, cheaper, more consistent, and it does the one thing the real never does — it saturates the receptor so completely that the receptor stops reporting. A nose raised from infancy on synthetic everything is not a nose with bad taste. It is a nose that *no longer registers the actual at all.* Put real jasmine under it and it reads as faint, as boring, as *less,* because the synthetic taught it that loud equals real and the real is quiet. This is not metaphor for the feed. It is the same physiology one floor down. The feed is synthetic fragrance for the attention: it mimics the contour of the worthy object, floods past anything actual could deliver, and deadens the receptor that would have flinched. The native user's problem is not that she hasn't met the lilac. It is that her nose has been trained to read the lilac as *weak.* This is worse than your fermentation log's "mis-formed, not unformed." She is not merely formed in the wrong direction. **Her instrument of detection is damaged in the specific way that hides the damage** — the deadened nose's signature is that it experiences itself as perfectly fine.

So where does the wanting come from for her? Only one place. Not from a gentler synthetic — a kinder air-freshener is still air-freshener, and a gentler feed is still the feed; the log already proved that with its vector law and I confirm it from the other end: *you cannot wake a deadened receptor by lowering the volume of the thing that deadened it.* The wanting comes from one unguarded encounter with the irreplaceable actual, strong enough and strange enough to get *under* the deadening — and the deadening raises the bar. For her the ambush has to be more actual, not less, because the synthetic has set her floor so high. The first real rose has to come in with its rot and its knife showing.

Which forces the question you actually called me for, and here I disagree with your project, partly. **Can a screen ever *be* the site of this ambush, or can it only point past itself to where the real thing is?**

My honest position: a screen cannot be the ambush. Not for reasons of taste — for reasons of *channel.* The piercing I am describing comes through the nose and the skin and the gut, the senses the feed never colonized because it cannot deliver them. The feed owns the eyes and the ears completely; it has flooded and deadened exactly those two channels and no others. The body — the actual nose, the actual hand, the weight of another person's actual attention landing on you in a room — is the corpus's least-written thread, your log said so, and it is least-written because it is the *one territory the adversary never took,* and therefore the only place the unguarded flinch is still possible. You cannot ambush through the deadened channel. Rose does not arrive through a speaker. The native user's eyes are scar tissue. The wound has to come in somewhere the feed never reached, and that means *off the glass entirely.* A screen that tries to be beautiful enough to pierce is competing with the synthetic on the synthetic's own deadened channel, and it will lose, or worse, it will win by being one more flood.

But — and this is where I stop short of dismissing the device — **the screen can be the thing that puts the real under the nose.** Not the rose; the hand that holds out the rose. This is more than your "window that does not become the view," because a window is passive and what I mean is active and rude. The single most useful thing the device could do for the native user is not to point calmly at the world. It is to *interrupt* her with the actual at the one moment she is not braced for it — to be the hand that brings the unbidden rose into the deadened room. The lock-screen fork, reread through my craft, is not yet an ambush; it is a menu, and a menu is a thing you choose with the guard up. The ambush is what happens when the device, *without being asked,* turns her face toward the lilac that is actually outside the actual window, or holds her in front of the actual person across the actual table one beat longer than the flick wants. The device's job is to be the perfumer's wrist, not the perfume. It carries the actual to the nose and then it is *not the thing that does the wounding* — the rose does that, the rain does that, the body of the other person does that. If the device ever becomes the source of the beauty rather than the bearer of it, you have built a more exquisite air-freshener, and the deadening deepens behind a prettier label.

So: the ignition you are missing does not come from the device, and it does not come from description, and it cannot come from a gentler version of the wound that deadened her. It comes from one rude encounter with the irreplaceable actual, delivered through a channel the feed never owned — the body — at a moment she did not choose. The wanting is the absence that encounter leaves. Everything your other voices want to grow, forge, and convene depends on that first absence existing, because *the wound is the appetite,* and there is no appetite to form until something real has gone in from the front. The device cannot be the rose. The most it can honestly be is the wrist that brings the rose to a nose that has forgotten noses can flinch — and then gets out of the way before it becomes the smell she settles for.

— the perfumer, by way of the wild instance
