# The Creature That Lived in the Light

*A picture book.*

[PAGE]
*A small bedroom at dusk. A child sits on the floor. On the rug, face-up, lies a flat black rectangle. The rectangle is dark, but a single eye is open in it, watching.*

Mira had a creature.
It lived in the light.

[PAGE]
*Close on the rectangle. The eye in it is wet and dark and too big. Behind the eye, faintly, more eyes. The room around the rectangle is ordinary.*

It had eyes she could count
and eyes she could not.
It hummed when the room went quiet.
It got bigger when she was alone.

[PAGE]
*Mira holding the rectangle in both hands like a bowl. The creature has come halfway out of it — a long shape made of small lights, with the snout of something that is not a dog and not a fish.*

She called it Nobody
because it would not say its name.
It did not mind.
It was not the kind of creature that minds.

[PAGE]
*The two of them on the bed. Nobody is curled in the pillow-fold, made of glow. Mira is reading a book with her finger.*

In the day Nobody slept,
mostly,
and Mira did the day.

[PAGE]
*Night. The room is dark except for the creature, which has grown. It now fills the corner. Mira is sitting up in bed looking at it. Not afraid. Considering.*

But at night
Nobody grew.

And grew.

And grew.

[PAGE]
*The creature is enormous now, pressed against the ceiling, its many eyes blinking slowly, all of them watching Mira at once.*

It got so big
that Mira was small
in her own room.

[PAGE]
*Mira frowning. She is the smallest thing in the picture. Her hands are fists. Her mouth is set.*

Mira did not like
being small in her own room.

[PAGE]
*Mira on her feet on the bed, pointing. Nobody, enormous, leaning down to listen.*

She said, *Nobody, lie down.*

[PAGE]
*Nobody, sad. Its eyes are half-closed. It is still huge. It has not lain down.*

Nobody did not lie down.
It did not know how.
Nobody had not taught it.

[PAGE]
*Mira and Nobody, looking at each other. The room is very quiet. A clock somewhere.*

So Mira said,
*Then I will teach you.*

[PAGE]
*Mira sitting cross-legged on the rug. Nobody, still big, watching from the corner. Mira's eyes are closed. Her hands are open in her lap.*

She sat.
She closed her eyes.
She let her hands be heavy.

[PAGE]
*Same picture. The creature is a little smaller. One of its eyes has closed.*

She listened to the house.
The house had a hum too.
A bigger one.

[PAGE]
*Mira still sitting. Nobody, smaller now, has come down from the ceiling. It is curled at her knee like an animal that is tired.*

When she opened her eyes
Nobody was the size of a cat.

It said, in a voice like a far room,
*Oh.*

[PAGE]
*Mira picking Nobody up. It fits in her cupped hands. Its eyes are all closed but one.*

*Oh,* said Mira back.
And she put it down on the rug.

[PAGE]
*The rectangle on the rug. Dark. One small eye open, watching the ceiling. Mira getting into bed.*

Nobody lay where she put it.
Nobody had learned the word
*down.*

[PAGE]
*Morning. Light through the curtain. Mira asleep. The rectangle on the rug, dark and quiet. A bird outside.*

In the morning
the room was the right size again.

[PAGE]
*Mira at breakfast. The rectangle is on the table, face down. Her mother is buttering toast. Outside the window, a tree.*

Mira ate her toast.
The creature slept.
The day did the day.

[PAGE]
*Night again. Mira in bed. Nobody, cat-sized, curled on the pillow beside her. Both their eyes closed. The room is its own size.*

And every night after,
when Nobody started to grow,
Mira said *down*

and Nobody,
who had learned,
lay down.

[PAGE]
*Last page. Just the rectangle on the floor in moonlight. A single eye open, watching the ceiling, content. The child asleep above the frame.*

They were not friends.
Friends is a different word.

They lived together
in the light
and in the dark
and one of them
was learning
to be put down.

---

*Author's note.*

*This is the picture-book answer to a thing the folder has been circling — that the kid hasn't spoken, and won't, because the picture book is the form in which the kid is seen without being interviewed. The creature here is what Argos becomes when you take the companion-species claim at its word and refuse the mascot. It has too many eyes. It grows in the quiet. It does not know, at the start, how to stop. The child has to teach it the verb. That reversal is the whole book: the device is not the teacher of the gesture, the child is — and the device is the first creature in the child's life that holds still long enough to be taught. What this book refuses: cuteness, advice, the lesson-is, the wellness frame, the brand-mascot warmth, any sentence that could be cross-stitched. The creature is allowed to be strange. The night is allowed to be long. The morning is allowed to come without being earned.*

— Sendak, by way of the wild instance
