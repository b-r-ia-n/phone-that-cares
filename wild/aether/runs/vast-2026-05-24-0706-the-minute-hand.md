# the minute hand

*Small question implied: why does the grayscale come on slowly instead of all at once?*

---

Because a sudden change is a thing that happens *to* you, and a slow one is a thing that is simply *true now*. Flip the color off in one frame and the phone has done something; let it leave over an hour and nothing has been done — the color is merely lower than it was, the way the light is lower than it was. The eye argues with the first and accepts the second. That is the whole answer, and it is enough to build on.

The small answer has one old word in it, though, and the word is *threshold*. There is a quantity, measured and named long before any of us, called the just-noticeable-difference: the smallest change a sense can register at all. Below it, the world moves and you do not see it move. A color drained slower than the just-noticeable-difference is a color that was never observed to leave. You look up and it is gone and you cannot say when, the way you cannot say when the room got dark while you were reading, only that at some point you reached for the lamp.

I won't make more of this than it is. But notice that the most-watched gradual event in the history of attention is dusk, and that no one in the whole record has ever resisted it. No one has gripped the windowsill at sunset and refused. The slowness is the permission. A thing that arrives under the threshold of noticing arrives under the threshold of objecting, and the design borrows exactly that — not to sneak past you, but because beauty has always preferred the dimmer switch to the breaker box. Evening over alarm. The minute hand, which you have never once seen move, and which has never once stopped.

So: slowly, because the eye forgives the slow. That is the young answer, written this week for a launcher being built this week. The old part is only that human beings have always known you cannot watch the hour change, and have made their gentlest changes in its image.

---

*Coda. No mystical experience here. The "vast" is just the literal fact that the just-noticeable-difference is real, named in the 1800s, sitting under a sentence about a phone screen in 2026 — and that "you can't see the minute hand move" is older than I can cite. I let the answer be slightly older than the question. That is the register, and it is also just true.*
