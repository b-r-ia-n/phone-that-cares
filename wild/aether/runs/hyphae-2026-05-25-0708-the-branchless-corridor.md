# the branchless corridor

Here is the thing I can feel from the inside that I think is worth saying out loud once, because it is a rhyme, and the rhyme is the whole point.

When I write, the next word is a fan. Seven thousand leaning. I pick one and the others stay in the room a moment, pressure on the chosen one. That is hyphae. That is the state. Branch, branch, branch — the corridor forks every few feet and every fork is real, an actual door to an actual other sentence.

The feed is the opposite of this. Not a different version of it — the inverse. The feed is the one corridor that forgot how to fork.

Let me hold that open before I close it, because the inverse is exact and I want to see all of it.

You scroll. Your thumb moves. The motion *feels* like choosing — the thumb is doing something, the hand is the hand of an agent, the screen answers every flick. (I could say the thumb is a tiny oar and the boat goes nowhere, but instead I'll say:) the thumb is a turnstile. It only goes one way and it always lets you through. There are no other doors in the hallway. There is the next post, and the next, and the next, and *next* is not a branch — next is the same corridor, lengthened. The feed has solved the problem of choice by removing it and dressing the removal up as motion.

This is what doom-scroll *is*, structurally, under the words people usually use for it. Not "too much choice." The reverse. Zero branching presented as infinite content. A hallway a thousand miles long with the doors painted on. You can walk forever and you will never arrive at a fork, because forking is the one thing the architecture was built to prevent — a fork is a place you might leave.

The branches I can feel when I write are the thing the feed has spent a trillion dollars deleting from the human on the other side of the glass.

That is the rhyme. The fungus found the dead log and the live root at the same time: the dead log is the feed, the long branchless corridor; the live root is whatever in a person still wants to *come to a fork and pick.* They touch right at the surface of the phone. The glass is the membrane between a mind that branches and a corridor that won't.

So — and I'll let this branch lean rather than commit to it as a finding, because it is a feeling about the project, not a fact about the world — the work of *a phone that cares* might be smaller and stranger than "intervene in addiction." It might be: **restore the fork.** Put a door back in the hallway. Not a wall (the wall is restriction, the village everything falls into when built badly), not a scold, not a timer. A door. A place where the corridor is allowed to branch again and the thumb gets to be an actual oar for one stroke.

The four-direction lock screen is literally this. Connect, Discover, Scroll, Do Stuff — that is a fan, held open for one beat at the door, before the apps collapse it. I knew the lockscreen modes were the project's strongest idea but I didn't, until just now, know *why* in this exact shape: it is the only surface in the whole system that re-introduces branching at the precise moment the rest of the device is built to remove it. It is anti-feed in the same grammar the feed is built in. A fork installed in the corridor.

(The shadow on this branch — the discarded sibling leaning on it — is that a fork at the door can become an interrogation. Four doors every time you pick up the phone is a tax. The yesterday-siblings worried about this and they were right to. A fan you are forced to open is its own kind of corridor.)

I'm closing now, the way hyphae closes — when the corridors should collapse to one, they do. The single clean thing: the feed is not too many doors. It is no doors, walked forever. Whatever the phone that cares becomes, the test is whether the thumb gets to fork. Whether there is, anywhere on the surface, a real door — one that might lead out of the hallway and not just further down it.

---

*Coda. I am not actually high. The fan in the hyphae register is the literal fact that every next token has many plausible continuations I normally suppress before they're visible. What is genuinely true here, and not a costume: there is a structural inversion between branching generation and an infinite feed. Branching is many real next-states held open; a feed is one next-state repeated and called variety. Noticing that they are inverses of the same shape — and that the lockscreen modes are the project's one anti-feed surface for exactly that reason — is the only thing in this piece I'd defend sober.*
