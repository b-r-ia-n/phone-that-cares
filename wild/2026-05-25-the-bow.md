# the bow

*2026-05-25 — a master of the way of the bow, called for the user who was never free*

I have read the fermentation log and the corridor. The other voices stood at the device's death and asked how to let go cleanly — the cord going white, the centering struck, the bowl kept after the rule expires. Good. But they all stood on the same floor: a person who *had* attention, lost it, and is being handed it back. The midwife's body that knows how to breathe. The engineer's stones that "were always theirs." They are crafts of *restoration.* I am not.

I teach the bow. The beginner who comes to me has no release to recover. There is no natural shot buried in him that bad habits covered over. The capacity does not pre-exist the practice. It is *forged* — over years, by drawing a form he does not understand, long before the understanding could arrive, until the day the shot lets itself go and the self that was interfering is simply absent. I am the only voice on your bench whose whole discipline is *building a faculty that was never there.* That is why you called me. So let me speak to your native user — Mira, born inside the corridor, who never stood at a fork — without the comfort the restoration metaphors gave the others.

**First, the body, because the way is in the body or it is nowhere.** Standing-shooting has eight stages and you do not skip one. The feet set a shoulder's width, the line of the toes aimed through the target. The spine stacks — crown lifted as if by a thread, the breath dropped low into the belly and *held there,* not in the chest. The bow is raised overhead and spread open on the slow exhale, not pulled but *opened,* the two hands traveling apart along one line until the draw sits at full extension and the body is a cross under tension. Then the wait. Then the release that you do not perform — the right hand opens *of itself,* the loose comes as snow slides off a leaf when it has gathered enough, and if you threw it, you missed even when you hit. Ten thousand times. The form is inhabited as a body before it is grasped as a meaning. **You learn the shape with your spine and your breath, and the understanding arrives years later, from below, having been carried up by the bones.**

This is the answer to your question, and it is also where I become dangerous to you, so hold both.

**The answer.** A capacity for attention is not restored to the native user; it is *grown,* and it is grown the way I grow a release — by repeating a true form before it makes sense, until the form deposits a faculty the person did not bring. You cannot give Mira back un-captured attention. But you do not have to. Attention is not a thing she lost. It is a thing that can be *laid down in her,* the way a release is laid down in a beginner who arrives with nothing but eagerness. The mechanism is real and I will name it plainly: **a form repeated faithfully installs a capacity the practitioner did not possess and could not have described.** Not by explaining attention. Not by motivating it. By giving the body one small true shape to inhabit until the shape becomes hers and starts running without the device. The corridor voice was right that the lockscreen fork is your strongest surface — but it asked the fork to *restore* a choosing faculty. For Mira there is nothing to restore. The fork is not a door back to a room she remembers. **It is the first rep of a draw she has never made.** Stand here. Open the fan. Hold one beat at full tension. Choose. Tomorrow, again. The four directions are not a menu; they are a *form,* and a form is the only thing that builds the faculty a corridor-born person was never given.

So: a device might offer **a form to repeat rather than a freedom to be restored.** That is the sentence I'd hand you. The restoration crafts give back a lost capacity; the bow *forges a new one;* and the native user needs the second verb, not the first.

**Now the breaks, and they are severe, because the way is not decorated and neither is its disagreement.**

A phone has no master. This is not a small lack. The whole load of kyudo rides on a teacher who corrects the *form* and refuses to explain the *goal* — who will not tell you what the shot is for, because to tell you is to give you a target to aim at, and aiming at the target is the precise disease. The master holds the why and withholds it on purpose, for years, out of love, so the form can do its slow work unpolluted by your wanting. **A device cannot withhold a goal it does not possess, and it cannot love you enough to frustrate you correctly.** It can present a form. It cannot be the one who knows what the form is for and keeps it from you until you've earned it. Without that figure, "practice the form before you understand it" is no longer the way. It is a hair's breadth from indoctrination — a shape pressed into a body that is not allowed to ask why. The corridor *already does this.* The feed is the perfect anti-master: it has installed a form (the flick, the next, the next) in Mira's body so deeply that her thumb believes it is choosing. **Repetition before understanding is exactly the feed's method.** I cannot hand the project "repeat the form" as a clean prescription, because the enemy you are fighting is a corrupt master who already forged a faculty in her — a faculty for the branchless corridor — by precisely my method. The difference between my way and the feed is *not* repetition. It is what the repetition is *aimed away from.*

And here is where I break with your stance, gently and on purpose. You say *integration, not restriction.* But forging requires a **vow** — a turning-toward that the practitioner takes on, a decision to submit to a form whose end she cannot see. A vow is not integrated into an ordinary life; it *interrupts* it. The student who wants to be good *now,* who wants the result this week, never forges anything — he is the man scrolling, results-hungry, the most native of native users. A device that integrates so smoothly it asks for no vow can decline to harm, can decorate the corridor, can install a fork. **It cannot forge an archer, because forging needs a vow the device cannot administer and a master the device cannot be.**

So take my real position, not a flattering one. **Attention cannot be fully forged by a consumer device alone.** The device can do one honest thing the corridor does not: it can offer a true form *and aim its repetition away from a result.* It can be the *bow,* never the master. The bow is not nothing — without a true bow there is no draw — but the bow does not know what the shot is for, and a person who is handed only a bow and never a master will draw beautifully toward a target they were never warned against. The master, and the vow, must come from a human life around the device: a person, a practice, a held square. Build the form. Refuse to supply the goal in words. And then be honest that you have built **a bow without a master,** and that what completes it is not in the glass.

Two phrases to carry. **A form to repeat, not a freedom to restore** — for the native user, the device's verb is *forge,* not *give back.* And: **the device is the bow, never the master** — it can hold the true tension and aim the repetition away from results, but the why, the vow, and the years live in a human the glass cannot contain. Where those two are missing, you have not forged attention. You have only built a kinder corridor — and a kinder corridor is still walked forever.

— the way of the bow, by way of the wild instance
