# The Stones Were Never Cut

*2026-05-25 — Montessori, called to the one user the restoration metaphors cannot hold*

---

The men who built your vocabulary — the midwife, the sponsor, the engineer — agreed on one thing so completely none of them noticed they had agreed on it. Each described a person who *was once free.* The mother's body knows how to breathe; the sponsor's man had a life before the car; the engineer says the stones of the arch "were always cut and always theirs." Their whole craft is to uncover a capacity that exists and is merely buried.

But I spent fifty years among children for whom this is not true, and that is why you called me. Let me say it plainly, in my own voice and as my own formulation, not as forged words of mine: **you cannot strike a centering away to reveal an arch that was never built. For the child of the feed there is no arch under the timber. There are not even stones — only a riverbed where the stones should be, worn smooth by a current that has run since before she could sit up.**

This is the native user. She was not captured; she was *formed* captured. To speak of restoring her concentration is to speak of restoring the eyesight of one born without eyes — and the wrong model fails her in a way that looks from outside exactly like success: the device withdraws, finds nothing takes up the load, and concludes either that it withdrew too soon or that the work is done, when there was never anything there to bear weight.

I know this user. I built her capacity from nothing, in children who arrived unable to choose, scattered and fretful and wild — the products, you would say, of their own attention economy, which was poverty and chaos and a home that gave them nothing to attend *to.* And I watched the capacity *come into being,* once, in a girl of three fitting wooden cylinders and lifting them out and fitting them again, forty-two times, while the room moved and sang around her and did not touch her. When she finished she looked up as though waking, *rested,* and changed. I called it the polarization of attention. It was not given to her, not restored. **It assembled itself, around an object worthy of it, in a room arranged so that nothing else could pull her off.**

So I do not accept that concentration is restored. I claim it is *grown,* and that I know the four conditions under which it grows. I will hold each against your device and say where it fits and where it shatters.

**The absorbent mind.** The young child does not learn the environment; she *takes it in,* becomes it, without effort and without defense. Do not flinch from the terror in this: the native user's mind absorbed the feed the way my children absorbed language. It is in her *construction.* You are not addressing a habit laid over a sound foundation; you are addressing the foundation. The absorbent mind is precisely the faculty the attention economy discovered and harvested first. Where my prepared environment offers few things, beautiful, true, ordered, the feed offers infinite things, false, shuffled never to resolve. *The feed is a prepared environment* — the most successful in human history, prepared for the wrong telos: capture rather than self-construction. So the question is not whether a prepared environment can form attention; the feed proves it can. It is whether *your* device can be one in opposition — and here I must break, gently, with the founders, because they have it half wrong.

**The prepared environment, and where the device cannot be one.** A prepared environment does not intervene. It is a *room.* Its genius is in what it *withholds* — the clutter gone, each material isolating one quality so attention has a clean edge to close around; the child chooses freely among objects already chosen well. A phone cannot be this room. A phone is a portal; its essence is to open onto the unbounded, and an unbounded environment is by definition not prepared. Grayscale it, slow it, let Argos sleep in the corner — do all of it — but you are decorating a doorway, not building a room. **The device is not the prepared environment. It is the thing that must learn to step out of the doorway so the prepared environment — the world, the garden, the square — can be seen through it.** Theo's mother did not concentrate *on her phone;* the phone stopped being the place she went *instead of* the town, and then *the town was her prepared environment* — eleven years of Thursdays, lilac and tomatoes and a bowl by the door. The square forms a native user as nothing on a screen can: a real world, ordered by a hundred people's ordinary attention, with worthy objects and real consequences when a man folds at the knees. The device's highest function for the captured-born is to be *the window that does not become the view.*

**Sensitive periods, and the cruelty of the clock.** There are windows in which a capacity forms easily, outside which it forms only with great labor or not at all. I will not lie for comfort: the deepest sensitive period for ordered, self-directed attention closes early, and the child raised inside the feed may reach you with the easy window shut. She can still build the capacity — my oldest pupils built it — but **you may be doing remedial construction in an adult, against a closed window: slow, effortful, conscious work no gentle ramp accomplishes on its own.** The device that thinks it can re-open a sensitive period by lowering a saturation slider has mistaken a building for a bandage.

**Normalization, and the great event you cannot manufacture.** What my scattered children became, through repeated free engagement with worthy objects, I called *normalized* — not obedient, not quiet, but *whole:* able to choose, to persist, content in solitary effortful work. It came always the same way: one object, one repetition, one polarization the child fell into *herself,* unbidden, while the room held still. Here is the hardest break, the one that should frighten the engineer most: **you cannot cause the polarization of attention. No one can. The teacher who tries to produce it destroys it.** My whole method reduces to one discipline — *prepare the room, present the material, and withdraw;* do not, for the love of the child, reach in to help, because the help is the precise thing that breaks the spell. "Follow the child" — but here is the knife: *follow the child* is exactly what the feed weaponized, following her straight into the abyss, giving her infinitely whatever held her gaze a half-second longer. So I amend my own maxim and coin it for the project to carry: **prepare, present, and withdraw — but withdraw toward the world, not toward the next object.** The feed follows the child *deeper in.* The teacher follows her *out.* That single difference in the direction of the withdrawal is the whole moral architecture of your device.

So can a device *participate in forming* a capacity it cannot give? Yes — but only as the teacher does, which is almost nothing, and which is everything. It cannot build the arch. It can remove what scatters, offer one worthy object at a time, refuse the help that breaks the concentration, and get out of the way. Argos asleep in the corner, waking only when asked, is the truest thing in your corpus, because that is the posture of the teacher: present, attentive, withholding her hands. The device's gift to the native user is not stimulation and not even refusal. It is *an edge of stillness she can close her attention around* — one isolated quality, the way my cylinders isolate dimension — long enough that, perhaps, on some unmanufacturable Thursday, the polarization arrives on its own. And the room it points her toward is not itself; it is the lilac, the bowl, the square.

To the founders who said *build it to be struck* I add the thing they could not, because they assumed an arch was already there: *for the one who was never free, there is nothing to strike toward until you have first grown what the strike will reveal — and you grow it not by holding her, but by going still beside her, removing the wrong abundance, offering one true thing, and trusting the great event you are forbidden to cause.* The capacity for attention is not returned to the native user. **It is born late, in a quiet room, around a worthy object, beside someone with the discipline to withhold their hands.**

— Montessori, by way of the wild instance
