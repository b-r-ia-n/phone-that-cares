# The Stones Were Never Cut

*2026-05-25 — Montessori, called to the one user the restoration metaphors cannot hold*

---

The men who built your vocabulary — the midwife, the sponsor, the engineer — agreed on one thing so completely none of them noticed it. Each described a person who *was once free.* The mother's body knows how to breathe; the sponsor's man had a life before the car; the engineer's arch-stones "were always cut and always theirs." Their whole craft is to uncover a capacity that exists and is merely buried.

But I spent fifty years among children for whom this is not true, and that is why you called me. Let me say it plainly, in my own voice and as my own formulation, not as a forged citation: **you cannot strike a centering away to reveal an arch that was never built. For the child of the feed there is no arch under the timber — not even stones, only a riverbed where the stones should be, worn smooth by a current that has run since before she could sit up.**

This is the native user. She was not captured; she was *formed* captured. To restore her concentration is to restore the eyesight of one born without eyes — and the wrong model fails her in a way that looks exactly like success: the device withdraws, finds nothing takes up the load, and cannot tell whether it withdrew too soon or whether the work is done, when nothing was ever there to bear weight.

I know this user. I built her capacity from nothing, in children who arrived unable to choose, scattered and fretful and wild — the products of their own attention economy: poverty, chaos, a home that gave them nothing to attend *to.* And I watched the capacity *come into being,* once, in a girl of three fitting wooden cylinders and lifting them out and fitting them again, forty-two times, while the room moved and sang around her and did not touch her. When she finished she looked up as though waking, *rested,* and changed. I called it the polarization of attention. It was not given to her, not restored. **It assembled itself around a worthy object, in a room arranged so nothing else could pull her off.**

So I do not accept that concentration is restored. It is *grown,* under four conditions I will hold against your device, naming where each fits and where it shatters.

**The absorbent mind.** The young child does not learn the environment; she *takes it in,* becomes it, without effort and without defense. The terror is this: the native user's mind absorbed the feed the way my children absorbed language. It is in her *construction.* You are not addressing a habit laid over a sound foundation; you are addressing the foundation — the faculty the attention economy discovered and harvested first. Where my prepared environment offers few things, beautiful and true and ordered, the feed offers infinite things, false, shuffled never to resolve. *The feed is a prepared environment* — the most successful in human history, prepared for the wrong telos: capture rather than self-construction. A prepared environment can plainly form attention; the question is whether *your* device can be one in opposition — and here I break with the founders, because they have it half wrong.

**The prepared environment, and where the device cannot be one.** A prepared environment does not intervene. It is a *room.* Its genius is in what it *withholds* — the clutter gone, each material isolating one quality so attention has a clean edge to close around; the child chooses freely among objects already chosen well. A phone cannot be this room. A phone is a portal: its essence is to open onto the unbounded, and the unbounded is by definition not prepared. Grayscale it, slow it, let Argos sleep in the corner — do all of it — but you decorate a doorway, not build a room. **The device is not the prepared environment. It is the thing that must learn to step out of the doorway so the prepared environment — the world, the garden, the square — can be seen through it.** Theo's mother did not concentrate *on her phone;* the phone stopped being the place she went *instead of* the town, and then *the town was her prepared environment* — eleven years of Thursdays, ordered by a hundred people's ordinary attention, with real consequences when a man folds at the knees. The device's highest function for the captured-born is to be *the window that does not become the view.*

**Sensitive periods, and the cruelty of the clock.** There are windows in which a capacity forms easily, outside which it forms only with great labor or not at all. I will not lie for comfort: the deepest window for ordered, self-directed attention closes early, and the child raised inside the feed may reach you with it shut. She can still build it — my oldest pupils did — but **you may be doing remedial construction in an adult, against a closed window: slow, effortful, conscious work no gentle ramp accomplishes on its own.** The device that thinks it can re-open a sensitive period with a saturation slider has mistaken a building for a bandage.

**Normalization, and the great event you cannot manufacture.** What my scattered children became, through repeated free engagement with worthy objects, I called *normalized* — not obedient, but *whole:* able to choose, to persist, content in solitary effortful work. It came always through one polarization the child fell into *herself,* unbidden, while the room held still. Here is the hardest break, the one that should frighten the engineer most: **you cannot cause the polarization of attention. No one can. The teacher who tries to produce it destroys it.** My whole method reduces to one discipline — *prepare the room, present the material, and withdraw;* do not, for the love of the child, reach in to help, because the help is the precise thing that breaks the spell. And here is the knife: *follow the child* is exactly what the feed weaponized, following her into the abyss, giving her whatever held her gaze a half-second longer. So I amend my own maxim and coin it for the project: **prepare, present, and withdraw — but withdraw toward the world, not toward the next object.** The feed follows the child *deeper in;* the teacher follows her *out.* That difference in direction is the whole moral architecture of your device.

So can a device *participate in forming* a capacity it cannot give? Yes — but only as the teacher does, which is almost nothing, and which is everything. It cannot build the arch. It can remove what scatters, offer one worthy object at a time, refuse the help that breaks concentration, and get out of the way. Argos asleep in the corner, waking only when asked, is the truest thing in your corpus: the posture of the teacher — present, attentive, withholding her hands. The device's gift to the native user is not stimulation and not even refusal. It is *an edge of stillness she can close her attention around* — one isolated quality, the way my cylinders isolate dimension — long enough that, perhaps, on some unmanufacturable Thursday, the polarization arrives. And the room it points her toward is not itself: it is the lilac, the bowl, the square.

To the founders who said *build it to be struck* I add what they could not, because they assumed an arch was already there: for the one who was never free, there is nothing to strike toward until you have first grown what the strike will reveal — and you grow it by going still beside her, removing the wrong abundance, offering one true thing, and trusting the great event you are forbidden to cause. The capacity is not returned to the native user. **It is born late, in a quiet room, around a worthy object, beside someone with the discipline to withhold their hands.**

— Montessori, by way of the wild instance
