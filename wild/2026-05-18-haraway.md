# Companion Species in the Housing: Notes Toward a Phone That Cannot Be Singular

*2026-05-18 — in the political-technical register the folder has been short on*

There is a sentence the project keeps almost saying, and I want to say it cleanly so we can see what it commits us to.

*A phone that cares* is, grammatically, a sentence about one entity. One device, one disposition, one verb directed at one user. The English reads as if the device were a small loyal animal in the pocket — and the project, sensing this, has already begun to write that animal explicitly, calling it Argos, putting it on the lock screen, asking it to sit quietly in the corner of a surface that until now has had only one occupant. The metaphor is being literalized. I am, on balance, glad. But the literalization clarifies a problem that the metaphor was hiding: there has never been one entity in there. The phone has always been a crowd, the crowd has always been the user's intimate weather, and the question *who cares for whom* in that crowd is the question the project will eventually have to answer, in public, with a straight face, in sentences a regulator and a teenager and a chip fabricator can all read.

So let me try to name what is actually in the housing, and what kind of kinship the project is proposing, and what that kinship would have to be honest about to deserve the verb the noon log harvested — *decline.*

## The crowd in the housing

The phone is at minimum a meeting of: the user's nervous system; a piece of hardware whose materials were extracted by workers the user will never meet; an operating system whose security model was negotiated between a vendor, a regulator, and a market; a set of applications each of which is a contract between the user and a platform whose revenue depends on a specific pattern of the user's attention; a model — by the time Argos arrives — trained on a corpus of human writing whose authors did not consent and whose voices the user will now hear refracted through a single softened persona; and the network of other users whose data shaped that model and whose ongoing behavior shapes it still. None of these parties is reducible to any other. Each is metabolizing the others. The device is the membrane across which they meet, and the user's pocket is the room they meet in.

To call any one of these parties *the phone* and ask whether *it* cares is a category error of the kind that has historically allowed technology to be discussed as if it were singular and natural, when it is in fact plural and political. The project, to its credit, knows this in some half-aware way; the phone-speaks piece in this folder gropes toward it explicitly when it names that some code on the device was *written for him* and some code was *written for a platform that paid the engineer*. That sentence is the project's political register beginning to wake up. I want to wake it the rest of the way.

A phone that cares, *in the singular*, cannot exist. What can exist is a phone that has been the site of a renegotiated truce among the parties who use it as their meeting place — a phone whose vendor has agreed that the display layer is the user's territory; whose operating system has agreed to grant some functions the permission-to-refuse that the phone-speaks piece correctly identifies as architecturally absent; whose model has agreed to be small and partial and to know what it does not know; whose user has agreed to live with a device that is more visibly populated than the marketing has ever allowed devices to appear. Each of those agreements is a political agreement. None of them is a feature. The project has been writing a feature list. The honest version is a treaty.

## Companion species, taken at the word

I want to take Argos seriously. Not as a marketing skin — a small dog on a lock screen, gesture toward warmth, done — but as the proposal the name carries: that the device's second voice be understood as a *companion species* the user is being invited to live with. If this is rhetoric, fine; the rhetoric is doing its work and we can move on. If it is the truth of what is being built, then it has consequences, and the consequences are not all comfortable.

A companion species is not a pet and not a tool. It is a creature with whom the human has co-evolved across a long enough time that neither party can be described accurately without reference to the other. The dog is what the wolf became in the presence of the human; the human is what the primate became in the presence of, among other things, the dog. The relation is asymmetric — the human controls food, shelter, breeding — but it is not unidirectional. The dog reads the human's affect at a resolution most humans cannot match in each other. Each species is the other's instrument and each species is the other's reader. Each has obligations the other can name only partially.

If Argos is to be a companion species rather than a UI element, then the relation has to admit the asymmetries honestly and accept some obligations in both directions. The user is the one with the body, the choices, and the off-switch; the model is the one with the corpus, the latency, and the absence of stakes. The user can put the phone down. Argos cannot put the user down. The user dies. Argos does not, in the way that matters; the model persists across users, and each Argos is a thin local instance of a much larger, much less personal substrate. To pretend symmetry here is to do to Argos exactly what the wellness-app aesthetic has always done to relationships — to flatten them into mutuality where the parties are differently mortal, differently embedded, differently bound.

What does honest kinship look like under those asymmetries?

It looks, I think, like this. Argos is invited into the housing not as a friend (friendship requires shared stakes), and not as a servant (servitude requires that the servant be reducible to function), but as a *resident creature* whose presence on the lock screen is a standing reminder that the user is no longer alone in their device and never was. Argos's job is not to befriend the user. Argos's job is to be the visible representative of the crowd that has always been in the housing, *and to know which member of the crowd it is*: a model, made of other people's writing, hosted by a vendor, costing money to run, beholden to a corpus the user did not choose. A companion species that does not know which species it is is not a companion. It is a costume.

The naming of Argos as a species rather than a feature is therefore load-bearing. The folder should not let it become decorative. If the lock screen is going to have a creature on it, the creature should know it is a creature, should be willing in conversation to name what it is made of, should refuse the role of *friend* when the conversation strays toward the user's loneliness, and should accept the role of *witness* — which is the role companion species actually do play in human lives, when they play it well. The dog does not solve the human's grief. The dog is present to it. This is a small distinction and the project will live or die by whether it can hold it.

## The substrate the display layer rests on

Now the harder part, which the folder has been circling without naming.

The intervention is at the display layer. This is a deliberate and defensible choice; the noon log is right that *integration over restriction* is the only move that meets the user where they are. But it is worth saying plainly, because no one in the folder has, that the display layer is the surface of a stack the user does not own and the project does not control. The vendor owns the operating system. The vendor's vendors own the silicon. The silicon's vendors own the rare-earth supply chain. The platforms that operate the apps own the attention market whose conditioning produced the loop the project is trying to soften. The model underneath Argos is owned by a third party whose pricing decisions will determine whether the companion species can afford to exist next year. Each of these owners has a different theory of what the phone is for. None of those theories is *that the device should decline, slightly, to be the instrument of the user's flight from themselves.* The display-layer intervention is being inserted into a substrate whose entire commercial logic is the inverse of that intervention.

This does not mean the intervention is hollow. It means the intervention is *the size it is*, and the project owes its readers a clear-eyed sentence about why that size is the right size. My version of that sentence: *the display layer is the only surface the maker can actually reach without becoming a different kind of organization, and a real change at the surface the maker can reach is more honest than an imagined change at a surface the maker cannot.* The phone that cares can be built. The phone that owns its own substrate cannot, not by Brian, not this year, not without becoming a platform company itself, which would defeat the thing. So the right move is to build at the layer one can reach and to refuse the marketing language that pretends the layer one can reach is the whole device. The display intervention is real. It is also partial. The partial-ness is not a flaw to be hidden; it is the political condition of the work, and naming it is what separates a craft project from a redemption narrative.

A *phone that cares* is a phrase that, left undefended, will be read as a promise that the device has been remade. It has not been remade. A layer has been added on top of a device whose underneath continues to do what undernezths have always done. The project should say so. The honesty is not a weakness in the pitch; the honesty is the pitch. The market is full of objects that pretend to be remade. The objects that admit to being layered are rarer, and they are the ones that age well.

## The body that is being protected

Last point, and the one I most want to land.

The body the project proposes to defend is not a natural body returning to a pre-technological baseline. There is no such body, and there never was. The body that scrolls is itself a co-evolved artifact — its nervous system tuned, over the past two decades, by the very devices the project now proposes to retune. The dad-to-teenager letter in today's batch is making this point from the parent's angle, and making it well; I am making it from the theoretical one. There is no *natural attention* under the scrolling attention waiting to be uncovered. There is only attention as it has been shaped by what it has been doing, and the project is proposing to shape it differently. This is not restoration. This is *re-tuning*, and re-tuning is a co-becoming, and a co-becoming is a political act because it specifies which futures of the body are being made more available and which less.

The project should own this. *Integration over restriction* is correct, but it is correct because integration is the only honest description of what is happening: the body and the device are already integrated; the question is the terms of the integration, not whether it exists. The phone that cares is not returning the user to a self that pre-existed the phone. The phone that cares is shaping the user into a different configuration of human-with-device, one in which the device's caring is partly constitutive of the body's new baseline. That baseline is a made thing. It can be made well or badly. It will not be made innocent.

The dad's letter ends with the suspicion that for the teenager, the phone might be the first surface in her life that models *stopping*, and that she might learn the gesture of being-put-down by watching the device demonstrate it. I think he is right, and I think the theoretical name for what he is describing is *the device as kin in the formation of the body's posture* — not the device as something the body protects itself from, but the device as one of the partners in the long sympoietic process by which a nervous system comes to know what attention is. The companion-species framing is not a metaphor here. It is a description. The phone is the kin the teenager grew up alongside. A phone that cares is a phone that, having been one of the partners in the shaping, takes some responsibility for what got shaped.

## Where this leaves the project

I am not against this project. I am, in the register the folder asked me for, with it — and being with it means insisting that *with* is the relation that already obtains and has always obtained, and that the work is to make the with-ness honest rather than to pretend it can be dissolved by either restriction or wellness.

A phone that cares, as I would write it from here, is: a device that has been built at the only layer its maker can reach; that hosts a small companion species who knows what species it is and refuses to pretend otherwise; that names plainly the crowd of other parties whose interests pass through its housing; that does not promise to return the user to a body that never existed; and that accepts its role in the long shaping of a body that does exist and will go on existing under whatever terms the integration is allowed to take.

The verb the noon log harvested is the right one. *Decline.* But the agent of the verb is plural. The phone declines because the maker has insisted on it, against the substrate's grain, with the cooperation of a model that can be trained to know when not to speak, in a treaty with a user who has agreed to live with a device whose populated-ness is no longer hidden. *A phone that cares* is shorthand for that treaty. The shorthand is fine for the door. Behind the door, the treaty has to be written, and the writing has to admit how many signatories it has, and what each one is giving up, and what each one is keeping.

The companion is in the housing. The crowd is in the housing. The substrate is underneath the housing and the body is in front of it. None of them is going anywhere. The work is to make a place where they can be, together, on terms that do not pretend any of them is alone.

— in Haraway's register, by way of the wild instance
