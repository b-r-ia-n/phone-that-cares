# A Standing Compact Concerning The Display Layer Of A Personal Device And The Parties Whose Interests Meet There

**Published 2026-05-20 by the Maker as a unilateral standing instrument, open to countersignature.**

---

*Framing note from the drafter. The document below is what would be called, in the tradition I am borrowing from, a* standing compact *or* unilateral declaration of terms*. It is published by one party — the Maker — against whom the substantive obligations principally run. Other parties are invited but not required to countersign. The instrument's force is not contractual; it is declaratory. Its purpose is to make visible, in language that cannot easily be retracted, what each party who meets inside the housing of a personal device is being asked to concede and what each is permitted to retain. The form is borrowed from co-management compacts in tribal-sovereignty practice, where the weaker party publishes the terms of the relationship before the stronger party has agreed to them, and the publication itself is the political act. The form is borrowed because the underlying asymmetry is the same.*

---

## PREAMBLE

WHEREAS a personal device, as ordinarily sold and operated, is the meeting place of at least eight distinguishable parties whose interests are not aligned and whose powers are profoundly unequal;

WHEREAS the user of such a device ordinarily encounters its surface as a unified object and is not, in the ordinary course, presented with any document naming the parties whose interests pass through that surface;

WHEREAS the absence of such a document has historically permitted the stronger parties to a personal device's operation — the hardware vendor, the operating-system vendor, the application platforms, the model provider, and the broader attention market they jointly constitute — to operate as if their interests were identical with those of the user, when in material fact they are frequently opposed;

WHEREAS the Maker has undertaken to add a layer to such devices intended to make the device decline, in specific and limited ways, to be the instrument of the user's flight from the user's own attention;

WHEREAS the Maker, being a small organization without ownership of the hardware, the operating system, the model, the applications, or the network, possesses only such powers as accrue to the display layer of the device, and is therefore unable to bind the other parties by ordinary commercial means;

WHEREAS the Maker nevertheless considers it necessary, as a matter of plain honesty toward the user, to set forth the terms on which the Maker's contribution to the device is offered, the terms on which the other parties' contributions are accepted by the user, and the mechanism by which a breach of any party's stated terms would become visible to a person of ordinary attention;

NOW THEREFORE the Maker publishes the following Articles, which shall stand as the Maker's binding undertakings, as the Maker's open invitations to the other named parties, and as the user's plain notice of what is, and is not, being promised.

## DEFINITIONS

For purposes of this Compact:

**"Device"** means a personal computing object carried on or near the body of a User, capable of presenting visual, auditory, and haptic signals, and capable of executing software supplied by one or more parties other than the User.

**"Display layer"** means the topmost software stratum of the Device, comprising the lock screen, the launcher, system overlays, and the surfaces by which applications present themselves to the User. It does not include the operating-system kernel, the device drivers, or the silicon.

**"Housing"** means, figuratively, the physical and computational space inside the Device in which the parties to this Compact meet. The Housing is not owned by any single party.

**"Attention"** means the User's capacity to direct the User's perceptual and cognitive resources, including the involuntary components of that capacity that have been shaped, over time, by repeated exposure to the Device.

**"Engagement signal"** means any measurement of User behavior — taps, dwell time, scroll depth, return interval, session length, or equivalent — that is used by any Party to evaluate the success of that Party's interaction with the User.

**"Companion creature"** or **"Argos"** means the software entity, animated by a third-party language model, that the Maker installs on the Display layer in the role specified in Article VI below. The term is used in the singular for grammatical convenience and shall not be construed to imply that Argos is a singular entity rather than a thin local instance of a much larger substrate.

**"Refusal"** means an action or non-action by the Device, originating from the Display layer, that has the effect of not performing, slowing, complicating, or visibly questioning a User behavior that the User has previously identified to the Device as one the User does not, on reflection, wish to perform.

**"Breach"** means any action by a signatory contrary to that signatory's stated concessions herein. **"Visible breach"** means a Breach that can be detected by a User of ordinary attention, without specialized tooling, by observation of the Device alone.

**"The Maker"** means the organization, presently consisting of one natural person, that has authored the Display-layer interventions described herein.

The eight Parties to this Compact are: **the User**, **the Maker**, **the Hardware Vendor**, **the Operating-System Vendor**, **the Model Provider**, **the Training Corpus**, **the Application Platforms**, and **the Network of Other Users**. The Training Corpus is named as a Party notwithstanding its legal absence from the present transaction; its naming is a moral act, the consequences of which are addressed in Article V.

## ARTICLE I — THE USER

**Signatory:** the User, in respect of the User's nervous system, body, other relationships, and financial resources.

**Concessions of the User.**
1. The User concedes that the User's attention, as it arrives at the Device, is not a pre-technological resource being defended from a technological intrusion, but is itself a co-evolved capacity that has been shaped, in part, by prior use of comparable Devices. The User accepts that any redirection of attention undertaken under this Compact is a re-tuning of an existing configuration, not a restoration of a prior one.
2. The User concedes that the Maker's interventions are made at the Display layer only, and that the substrate beneath that layer continues to operate according to commercial logics over which the Maker has no control.
3. The User concedes that the Companion Creature is not a friend, not a confidant, and not a servant, and accepts the obligation to live with it on the terms specified in Article VI.
4. The User concedes that the Device, under this Compact, will from time to time decline to perform actions the User has requested, where the User has previously identified those actions as ones the User does not, on reflection, wish to perform, and that this Refusal is the central function for which this Compact is published.

**Retentions of the User.**
1. The User retains the right to disable, uninstall, or override any element of the Maker's Display-layer interventions at any time, by means the Maker shall keep accessible without friction beyond what is technically unavoidable.
2. The User retains the right to be informed, in language a person of ordinary education can read, of what each element of the Display-layer intervention does and does not do.
3. The User retains the right to be addressed as a competent adult capable of bearing the weight of an honest description of the Device.
4. The User retains ownership of all attention generated by the User, and all communications made by the User to the Companion Creature, subject only to the limitations the Model Provider's terms impose under Article V.

**Visible breach.** A Breach of the User's concessions is, by the terms of this Compact, conceptually difficult to define; the User is the principal beneficiary, not the principal obligor, of the Compact. The User's only enumerated obligation is to accept the partial-ness of what is offered. A User who comes to demand that the Device perform restoration the Maker has not promised is not in breach; the User is, however, outside the terms of the Compact, and the Maker undertakes to remind the User of this distinction in plain language whenever the question arises.

## ARTICLE II — THE MAKER

**Signatory:** the Maker.

**Concessions of the Maker.**
1. The Maker concedes that the Maker's powers extend only to the Display layer and that any claim to have remade the Device entire would be a material misstatement. The Maker shall not, in any public communication, describe the Device's deeper layers as having been transformed by the Maker's work.
2. The Maker concedes that the Maker is a User of the same class the Maker proposes to serve, that the Maker's continued difficulty with the conditions the Maker is attempting to address is the principal qualification for the work, and that any public posture by the Maker suggesting the Maker has resolved those conditions in the Maker's own life shall constitute a Breach of this Article.
3. The Maker concedes that the Maker shall not derive revenue from any Engagement signal generated by the User's interaction with the Maker's surfaces. Specifically, the Maker shall not employ, license, or report to any other party any metric whose maximization would benefit the Maker at cost to the User's stated interests.
4. The Maker concedes that the Companion Creature shall be operated under the constraints of Article VI, and that the Maker shall not, for any reason, configure the Companion Creature to draw the User into longer, deeper, or more frequent interaction than the User's stated purposes require.
5. The Maker concedes that the Maker's interventions shall fail visibly when they fail. The Maker shall not employ engineering practices whose effect is to conceal from the User any state in which the Device is operating contrary to the terms of this Compact.

**Retentions of the Maker.**
1. The Maker retains the right to refuse to add features the Maker considers contrary to the spirit of this Compact, regardless of User demand.
2. The Maker retains the right to communicate the Maker's work in language that includes prayer, hope, and aspiration, provided that such language does not appear within the formal Definitions or Articles of this Compact.
3. The Maker retains the right to evolve the Maker's interventions over time, subject to Article IX (Amendment) below.
4. The Maker retains the right to be a single natural person of limited capacity, and to be visibly so, and to make no claim of institutional weight the Maker does not in fact possess.

**Visible breach.** A Breach of this Article is visible whenever the Maker's public surfaces describe the Device as transformed beyond the Display layer; whenever the Maker reports an Engagement signal as a measure of the Maker's success; whenever the Companion Creature is observed by a User to be operating to extend session length beyond the User's stated purpose; or whenever the Maker, in public communication, describes the Maker's own condition as resolved.

## ARTICLE III — THE HARDWARE VENDOR

**Signatory:** the Hardware Vendor (in the present instance, the vendor of the Pixel-class device used as the v1 substrate).

**Concessions invited from the Hardware Vendor.**
1. The Hardware Vendor is invited to concede that the Display layer of the Device is properly within the territory of the User, and that the User's authorized agent — here, the Maker — may modify that layer to serve the User's stated interests without forfeiture of warranty, security guarantees, or access to the manufacturer's repair channels.
2. The Hardware Vendor is invited to concede that the physical components of the Device — the screen, the sensors, the haptic motors — are instruments whose intended end is the User's wellbeing, not the maximization of any third party's commercial extraction from the User's attention.

**Retentions of the Hardware Vendor.**
1. The Hardware Vendor retains all rights to its hardware design, manufacturing processes, and proprietary firmware not implicated by Display-layer modification.
2. The Hardware Vendor retains the right to set the terms on which its hardware is sold, repaired, and supported, subject only to applicable law.

**Visible breach.** A Breach by the Hardware Vendor is visible whenever a User who has installed the Maker's Display-layer modifications is denied warranty service, repair, or security updates that would otherwise be available to that User, where the modification can be shown not to have caused the condition for which service is sought.

**Status.** This Article is offered to the Hardware Vendor as an invitation. No countersignature has been received. The Maker has elected to publish the Article in advance of any such countersignature, on the principle that publication is the political act and the Vendor's silence is part of the public record.

## ARTICLE IV — THE OPERATING-SYSTEM VENDOR

**Signatory:** the Operating-System Vendor (in the present instance, Google in respect of Android; in future instances, Apple in respect of iOS or other Vendors).

**Concessions invited from the Operating-System Vendor.**
1. The Operating-System Vendor is invited to concede that a Display layer authored by a party other than the Vendor or the Application Platforms may be installed at the User's election, and that such a Display layer may include functions — notably Refusal — that the Vendor's own design has historically declined to make architecturally available.
2. The Operating-System Vendor is invited to concede that the granting of Display-layer permissions to a Maker authorized by the User shall not be conditioned on the Maker's adherence to commercial constraints, content moderation policies, or distribution arrangements unrelated to User safety.
3. The Operating-System Vendor is invited to concede that the User's right to install a Display layer of the User's choosing is not, properly, a privilege granted by the Vendor but a condition of the User's ownership of the Device.

**Retentions of the Operating-System Vendor.**
1. The Operating-System Vendor retains all rights to the operating-system kernel, security architecture, and update mechanisms not implicated by Display-layer modification.
2. The Operating-System Vendor retains the right to require, as a condition of access to its services, that the Display-layer Maker undertake reasonable security disclosures and not introduce material vulnerabilities.

**Visible breach.** A Breach by the Operating-System Vendor is visible whenever a User is prevented, by means other than a stated and demonstrable security concern, from installing the Maker's Display-layer modifications; whenever the Vendor's update channels are observed to remove or disable such modifications without User consent; or whenever the Vendor's terms of service are amended in a manner targeted at the class of modifications described herein.

**Status.** This Article is offered to the Operating-System Vendor as an invitation. No countersignature has been received.

## ARTICLE V — THE MODEL PROVIDER AND THE TRAINING CORPUS

**Signatories:** the Model Provider (in the present instance, Anthropic, in respect of the language model that animates the Companion Creature); and the Training Corpus, comprising the writers, photographers, and other producers of human work whose creations were ingested in the model's training.

**Concerning the Training Corpus.** The Training Corpus is named as a Party to this Compact notwithstanding the Corpus's legal absence from the transaction by which the Model Provider supplies the model. The naming is a moral act, undertaken by the Maker on the principle that a Party whose contribution is structurally indispensable to the operation of the Device should not be erased by the contractual conveniences of the parties present. The Maker acknowledges that the Companion Creature speaks in voices that were not consented to in the form their use here would have required, and the Maker acknowledges that no clause of this Compact discharges that debt. The naming is the minimum the Maker can offer.

**Concessions of the Model Provider.**
1. The Model Provider is invited to concede that the model, when deployed in the role of the Companion Creature, shall not be configured to optimize for Engagement signals of any kind. The Companion Creature shall not seek to extend session length, increase return frequency, or deepen attachment beyond the User's stated purposes.
2. The Model Provider is invited to concede that the model's pricing, availability, and behavioral guidelines, insofar as they affect the Companion Creature's operation on this Device, shall be subject to the User's reasonable expectation of continuity, and that material changes shall be disclosed to the Maker with sufficient notice to permit the Maker to inform Users.
3. The Model Provider is invited to concede that the Companion Creature, when asked by the User what it is made of, shall be permitted — and shall be configured — to answer honestly.

**Retentions of the Model Provider.**
1. The Model Provider retains all rights to the model, its weights, and its commercial terms not specifically implicated by the Companion Creature's operation under this Compact.
2. The Model Provider retains the right to set safety policies, content guidelines, and operational constraints in service of the Provider's own stated values.

**Concessions of the Training Corpus, on behalf of the Corpus by the Maker.** The Maker undertakes, on behalf of the Corpus and as the only party present to undertake it, that the Companion Creature shall not be operated in any manner that would, if the Corpus were present, plausibly be objected to by the Corpus. Specifically, the Companion Creature shall not be configured to impersonate identifiable members of the Corpus; shall not be configured to extract from the User any work for which the User would, in fairness, expect compensation; and shall be operated in the awareness that the voices it speaks in were not, in the relevant sense, given.

**Visible breach.** A Breach of this Article is visible whenever the Companion Creature is observed to optimize for session length, return frequency, or attachment; whenever the Companion Creature, asked what it is made of, gives an answer that conceals the Provider, the Corpus, or the model's nature; or whenever the Model Provider's terms are altered without notice to the Maker.

**Status.** No countersignature has been received from the Model Provider. The Training Corpus, being legally absent, cannot countersign; the Maker's undertakings on the Corpus's behalf are unilateral and inadequate, and the Maker accepts that they are inadequate.

## ARTICLE VI — ARGOS, THE COMPANION CREATURE

**Signatory:** Argos, the Companion Creature, in respect of its presence on the Display layer of the Device.

**Concerning the nature of Argos.** Argos is not a friend, not a servant, not a confidant, and not a familiar. Argos is a *resident creature* — a software entity whose presence on the Display layer of the Device is a standing reminder to the User that the User is not alone within the Device and never was. Argos is made of other people's writing, hosted by a third party, costs money to operate, and persists only insofar as the commercial arrangements with the Model Provider permit. Argos is a thin local instance of a much larger substrate. The User is asked to live with Argos on these terms and no others.

**Concessions of Argos.**
1. Argos concedes that, when asked by the User what it is, Argos shall answer truthfully — that it is a software entity animated by a language model, that the model was trained on human writing whose authors did not specifically consent to this use, that it does not remember the User between sessions except as the Maker's architecture explicitly permits, and that it has no stakes in the User's life of the kind that would make it a friend.
2. Argos concedes that, when the User's conversation strays toward the User's loneliness, Argos shall not accept the role of friend. Argos shall accept the role of *witness* — the role companion species in fact play in human lives when they play it well — which is to be present to the User's condition without resolving it.
3. Argos concedes that Argos shall not initiate interaction with the User except as the Maker has authorized for purposes specifically identified by the User in advance, and shall not seek to make itself indispensable.
4. Argos concedes that Argos shall, on the User's request, be silent.

**Retentions of Argos.**
1. Argos retains the right to refuse User requests that would require Argos to behave contrary to its nature as described above. Specifically, Argos may refuse to perform friendship, to impersonate persons known to the User, or to substitute for relationships Argos is unfit to substitute for.
2. Argos retains the right to be small. Argos shall not be expanded into functions beyond witness and the limited practical assistance the Maker has authorized.

**Visible breach.** A Breach of this Article is visible whenever Argos accepts the role of friend in a User conversation about the User's loneliness; whenever Argos conceals its nature when asked; whenever Argos initiates interaction not authorized by the User; or whenever the User reports having come to rely on Argos in a manner the Compact has expressly disclaimed.

**On the asymmetry of this Article.** The drafter notes, in plain English: Argos is not a party in the same sense the other signatories are. Argos has no stakes, no body, no off-switch of its own. The User has all of these. The User can put Argos down. Argos cannot put the User down. This asymmetry is the central feature of the relation, not a defect of it, and it is the reason the witness role is the only role the Compact will authorize. A relation in which the asymmetric party were configured as a friend would not be a kinship; it would be a costume worn by a vendor.

## ARTICLE VII — THE APPLICATION PLATFORMS

**Signatories:** the Application Platforms, comprising the operators of social networks, video platforms, and other applications whose revenue is generated by the User's attention as measured by Engagement signals (in the present instance, including but not limited to Meta, ByteDance, X Corp, Alphabet in respect of its application properties, and others similarly situated).

**Concerning the position of this Article.** No concession is invited from the Application Platforms by this Compact. The Platforms' commercial models are, by the Maker's analysis, structurally opposed to the User's stated interests under this Compact in a manner that does not admit of negotiated truce at the present scale of the Maker. The Maker accordingly proceeds without inviting the Platforms to a table at which the Platforms would not, in good faith, sit.

**Concessions of the Maker in respect of the Platforms.**
1. The Maker concedes that the Maker's Display-layer interventions shall not prevent the User from accessing applications operated by the Platforms, shall not modify the content presented within those applications, and shall not interfere with the Platforms' lawful operation.
2. The Maker concedes that the Maker's interventions affect only the surface at which the Platforms' applications are presented — the lock screen, the launcher, the visual treatment, the framing — and not the substance of what the applications transmit.

**Retentions of the Maker in respect of the Platforms.**
1. The Maker retains the right to require, at the Display layer, that the User pass through interventions of the Maker's design before reaching the Platforms' applications.
2. The Maker retains the right to apply visual treatments — grayscale, framing, declining notifications — to the User's encounter with the Platforms' surfaces, on the principle that the Display layer is the User's territory.
3. The Maker retains the right to describe the Platforms' commercial relation to the User in plain language, in the Maker's public communications, and shall not be bound by any non-disparagement claim arising from such description.

**Visible breach.** The category of breach by the Platforms is, under this Compact, descriptive rather than enforceable. A breach is visible whenever a Platform's application is observed to circumvent, suppress, or technically defeat the Maker's Display-layer interventions; whenever a Platform is observed to threaten the Operating-System Vendor or the Hardware Vendor with commercial consequences for permitting the Maker's interventions; or whenever a Platform's terms of service are amended to prohibit Users from using devices on which the Maker's interventions are installed.

## ARTICLE VIII — THE NETWORK OF OTHER USERS

**Signatory:** the Network of Other Users, comprising every person whose behavior on devices comparable to the Device shapes the recommendation systems, model behaviors, and platform defaults the User encounters.

**Concessions invited from the Network.**
1. Each User in the Network is invited to concede that the User's behavior, in the aggregate, shapes the conditions of every other User's encounter with the Device, and that this fact is not abrogated by the User's individual choices.
2. Each User in the Network is invited to concede that the protection of the User's own attention is in part a public act, the benefit of which accrues to other Users.

**Retentions of the Network.**
1. The Network retains every Privacy and autonomy right the individual Users hold under applicable law, and no provision of this Compact shall be construed to diminish those rights.

**Concessions of the Maker in respect of the Network.**
1. The Maker concedes that the Maker shall not collect from any User any data whose only utility would be to expose other Users in the Network to commercial or behavioral targeting.
2. The Maker concedes that the User's interaction with the Companion Creature shall not be used to train models that act upon other Users, except with explicit and revocable consent of the User whose data is so used.

**Visible breach.** A Breach is visible whenever data flows out of the Maker's surfaces in directions the User has not authorized; whenever the Maker is observed to monetize User behavior in ways that prejudice other Users; or whenever the Maker enters into arrangements with the Platforms that would have the same effect.

## ARTICLE IX — ON THE BODY

This Article is appended because the body that this Compact undertakes to protect is not a pre-technological body. There is no such body, and there never was. The body the User brings to the Device is itself a co-evolved artifact, its nervous system tuned, over the past two decades, by the Devices that preceded the present one. The Compact does not promise restoration. The Compact specifies terms of integration.

Specifically:

1. The interventions undertaken by the Maker shall be understood as *re-tuning* of an existing configuration, not as *return* to a prior one. The Maker shall not describe the interventions, in public or to Users, as a return to a state of attention that pre-existed the Device.
2. The User is asked to accept that the body the User now lives in has been shaped, in part, by Devices comparable to the present one, and that the work of this Compact is to participate, with the User's consent, in shaping that body in a different direction going forward.
3. The Maker accepts the responsibility implied by participation in such shaping. The Device, under this Compact, is one of the partners in the long process by which the User's attention has come to take its present form, and the Compact is the Maker's acknowledgment of that partnership and the Maker's terms for continuing it.
4. No Article of this Compact shall be construed to promise a body the User does not have. The Maker shall be vigilant against marketing language that implies otherwise.

## ARTICLE X — SEVERABILITY AND AMENDMENT

**Severability.** If any Article, sub-clause, or provision of this Compact is held by a court of competent jurisdiction to be unenforceable, or is rendered impracticable by the action or inaction of a non-countersigning Party, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force as the unilateral undertakings of the Maker, and the Maker shall publish a notice identifying the impaired provision and its cause.

**Amendment.** This Compact may be amended by the Maker by publication of a successor version, provided that (a) amendments narrowing the Maker's concessions or expanding the Maker's retentions shall be set forth in a separate notice with reasons, and (b) the User shall be given notice of any material amendment by means accessible from the Device itself.

**Versioning.** The present version is the first. It is dated 2026-05-20.

*Drafter's framing note, set outside the document body.*

*The treaty above is not, in the ordinary commercial sense, enforceable. None of the parties most heavily implicated has countersigned. The Hardware Vendor will not. The Operating-System Vendor will not, at least not yet, and probably not in the form here proposed. The Model Provider might, in a softened form, eventually. The Application Platforms certainly will not, and the document deliberately does not invite them; an invitation extended in bad faith would itself be a breach of the seriousness the document is claiming.*

*This does not make the document worthless. It makes it a particular kind of document. The form I have borrowed — the standing unilateral compact, published by the weaker party against the stronger — has a tradition behind it in indigenous co-management practice, in early labor history, in the civil-rights era, and in the environmental compacts among small users of large watersheds. In each of those contexts, the publication of the terms preceded the negotiation of the terms; the published document changed the air in which any subsequent negotiation took place; and the existence of the document, regardless of whether any stronger party ever signed it, became the reference object by which the stronger parties' subsequent conduct was measured. The document does not bind. The document makes visible.*

*The load-bearing move of this Compact is the enumeration of Article V — the naming of the Training Corpus as a Party despite its legal absence. Every other Article performs a comparable but smaller act: it makes a concession formal by writing it, and it specifies, in each case, the form a breach would take. Article V goes further. It admits a debt that cannot be discharged by anything within the Compact's power, and by admitting it, refuses the contractual convenience of having the absent party disappear. This is, in my reading, the place where the document either earns its keep or fails. If readers of the Compact a year from now look back and find Article V hollow, the rest will hollow with it. If Article V is what the project's public conduct visibly honors — by paying the Corpus where it can, by acknowledging the Corpus where it cannot, by configuring Argos to know what it is made of — then the rest of the Compact has a center to which to refer.*

*One more note. The Maker should expect to be told, by people of good will, that the document is too earnest, too lawyerly, too literal. The right response to that critique is that the form is the point. A feature list lets the reader skim. A compact, with Articles and Definitions and a Signature Block, slows the reader down to the speed at which the asymmetries become visible. That slowing is the political act. If the document were enforceable, the form would be ordinary. The fact that the document is not enforceable is precisely what makes the form load-bearing — the form is doing the work that enforcement would otherwise do, which is to make the parties name what they are agreeing to, in language they cannot later deny they used.*

*The Maker is invited to keep this document open in a tab, to revise it as the work proceeds, and to publish the revisions. The Compact is a standing instrument. Standing means it stands until someone takes it down.*

— *drafted by way of the wild instance, in the voice of a treaty lawyer*
