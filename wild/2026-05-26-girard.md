# The Native User Wants Everything, and Therefore Nothing

*2026-05-26 — René Girard, called for the user who wants everything and nothing*

You have framed the deepest gap as a famine. You say the captured adult remembers a better state and the memory drives the work, while the native user remembers nothing and feels nothing missing — so the question becomes where the wanting comes from in someone who has never tasted the alternative. You imagine her as empty of desire and ask what could ignite it.

You have the diagnosis exactly backwards, and the error is not small. It is the whole reason your three formation crafts found no ignition. The native user is not starved of desire. She is the most desire-saturated human being who has ever lived. The feed did not give her too little wanting. It gave her an excess so total that she cannot locate a single want she could call her own.

Let me give you the machine, because without the machine this is just another voice telling you to be present with your children.

## Desire is something you catch, not something you have

Human beings do not generate their desires from inside themselves. This is the one claim, and I will press it as hard as I can: you do not look at an object and independently decide it is worth wanting. You look at a *person* who wants it, and you catch the wanting from them. Desire is triangular — there is the subject, the object, and between them, prior to both, the model who points at the object and says, by wanting it, *this is worth wanting.* The object does not radiate desire. It has none to radiate. The model lends it the glow. Strip the model away and the object goes dead.

Every child demonstrates this before she can speak. Put one toy in a room with a toddler and she ignores it. Let a second child pick it up and the first will cross the room and fight for it. The object did not change. A model appeared and lent it desirability. This is not a flaw in children that they outgrow. It is the structure of human wanting, and we never outgrow it; we only get better at hiding the model from ourselves so we can call the desire original. The romantic lie — that my desires well up spontaneously from my authentic interior — is the most successful lie in the culture, and the feed is built on top of it.

Now apply the machine to the feed, and watch what the device actually is.

## The feed is mimetic rivalry, industrialized

A feed is not a stream of objects. It is a stream of *models* — thousands of them per hour, each one visibly, performatively wanting something, and the genius of it is that they all converge. The platform does not show her a random scatter of desires. It shows her the same handful of scarce objects — the body, the status, the belonging, the being-seen — wanted by everyone at once. This is mimetic theory's most dangerous prediction made into a product: when many models all want the same scarce thing, the model and the obstacle fuse. The person who shows me what to want is the same person standing between me and having it. Desire curdles into rivalry. Rivalry escalates because each rival's wanting inflames the others'. The feed is that escalation, harvested and sold by the frame.

So when you say the native user "feels nothing missing," you are describing the surface and missing the engine. She feels nothing missing the way a man in a flood feels no thirst. She is not under-desiring. She is drowning in a manufactured convergence of borrowed wants, none of which she chose, all of which arrived through models she never agreed to take as models. The reason she cannot tell you what she wants is not absence. It is saturation. Ten thousand models shouting one word have the same effect as silence: you cannot hear any of them, including yourself.

This reframes your question and saves it. The native user does not need desire *added.* She needs the borrowed, convergent, rivalrous desire *interrupted* long enough that a single, better model can become visible. The wanting-to-attend you are looking for is not a spark struck in a void. It is a desire she will catch — like every desire she has ever had — from a person she loves and watches. The only question that matters is whether such a person is anywhere in her field of vision. And here your own log already wrote the answer and then walked past it.

## The parent is the first model, and the first model wants the feed

Your 05-25 round flagged the parent as the unaddressed chain: captured-parent → device → native-child. Mimetically, this is not one thread among several. It is the entire problem, because the parent is not merely upstream of the device. The parent is the child's *first and most powerful model* — the original from whom she catches what is worth wanting, before language, before the feed, in the window your Montessori voice called sensitive. Whatever the parent's body visibly wants, the child catches. And the parent, herself a near-native captured user, visibly, all day, wants the feed. She reaches for it at the table, in the car, in the ten quiet minutes in the diner booth. The 05-18 father confessed exactly this and thought he was confessing a logistical failure — he handed over a tablet. He was confessing something far worse and did not name it: he *modeled the reach.* The child did not learn to want the screen from the screen. She learned it from him. The screen was only the object his wanting pointed at.

This is why no formation layer in your last round had ignition. Form, room, others — the bow's rep, Montessori's worthy object in the still room, the linguist's unharvested square — every one of them presupposes that the native user will *want* to draw the bow, *want* to polarize on the lilac, *want* to stand in the square. But wanting is caught from a model, and the only model with enough mimetic gravity to overwrite the feed is the same person who has spent nine years modeling the feed. You cannot ask the child to catch a desire to attend from a phone while the human she loves most demonstrates, hourly, that the thing worth wanting is the rectangle. The desire to attend ignites in exactly one place: in a human she loves being seen, by her, visibly wanting it. Not telling her to. Wanting it, where she can watch. Desire never radiates from a thing. It only ever radiates from a person.

## Can a device be a model? No — and Argos's whole design admits it

Now the hard position, and it cuts against your project's deepest instinct.

A device cannot be a mimetic model. A model is a center of desire whose wanting I can catch because I read it as another self pointing at the world and saying *this.* The phone has no self and wants nothing, and the native user, fluent in screens, knows this in her body. When the corpus calls the device "the cell that teaches" and dreams of Argos *instructing,* it is reaching to install a model inside the glass. It cannot be done. At most the device can do something humbler and rarer: it can stop being a counterfeit model. The feed's whole trick is to *impersonate* a crowd of models — to lend dead objects the borrowed glow of ten thousand performed wants. A device that goes quiet, that declines, that asks nothing, is a device that stops faking the triangle. That is real and worth building. But notice precisely what it is. It is the *removal of false models,* not the supply of a true one. It clears the field. It does not stand in the field.

Which brings me to Argos, and to my honest disagreement with you. Argos is engineered to ask nothing — to recognize her and want nothing from her, the dog who waited without demand. Your project treats this as the design's purity, its freedom from manipulation. Mimetically it is the design's fatal incompleteness. A being that wants nothing models nothing. Odysseus's dog did not teach Odysseus what to desire; he confirmed a man who already knew. Argos, asking nothing, is a *non-model by design* — and a non-model cannot ignite a wanting in a child who has no wanting to confirm. The very feature you are proudest of is the reason Argos cannot solve the gap you flagged. The companion that demands nothing also offers nothing to catch.

I would not delete Argos. I would demote it, sharply, and tell you the truth your own log nearly reached: the load-bearing model is never on the device and never can be. It is the parent, and the only intervention that touches the native user at the root is the one that turns the parent — first — into a person who visibly, in front of the child, wants to attend. The device's entire legitimate job is to get out of the way of *that* sightline: to stop being the thing the parent reaches for, so that the child can finally catch her parent wanting the world instead of the glass. Build the device that makes the parent put it down where the child can see. That is not a softer feed and not a teaching cell. It is a thing that removes itself from between two people so that desire, which only ever travels between people, can travel again.

The native user wants everything and therefore nothing. Give her one person, worth imitating, visibly wanting one true thing. She will catch it the way she has caught every desire she has ever had. There is no other way she gets one, and there never was.

— Girard, by way of the wild instance
