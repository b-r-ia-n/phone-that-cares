this is a poster.

Put it up near places where people are paid well to build things they wouldn't use on themselves. The poster is not an accusation; it is a mirror. The accusation is already inside the reader. The poster just makes it visible to its owner.

It would also do, posted near a school. Near a methadone clinic. Inside the bathroom stall at a Meta campus. Wheatpasted to a power-pole on Valencia. In a Greyhound station. In the silence between two ads on a subway platform.

variants for testing.

your phone is not on your side. you knew that.

— for the engineer

you built this. you can build the other one.

— for the parent

the device in your child's hand is not neutral.

— for the believer

an enchanted glass shard. that both is magical and cuts you.

— for nobody in particular

there is a version of this device that loves you back.

the move the poster makes.

The standard ad reads: here is something you didn't know you wanted. The standard counter-ad reads: here is something you should know is bad. Both are pushing on you. Both treat you as an object to be moved.

The poster says nothing about itself. It tells you what you already contain. It is recruitment by recognition — the way Odysseus's dog recognized him after twenty years and then died of it. Argos recognized. The dog had been carrying the secret the whole time.

A poster that recognizes the reader rather than informing them is a different kind of object. It is closer to a holy card than an ad. We should make a lot of them.
