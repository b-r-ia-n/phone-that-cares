# Proverbs of the Phone

*Written in the wild room, near Pentecost, in the year the tavern fit in a pocket. Take these as one takes a stone from a riverbed — wet, weighted, indifferent to whether you carry them home.*

---

1. The infinite feed was built by men who fled their own attention; they have made of their flight a public thoroughfare.

2. Where the algorithm calls itself neutral, there sits a god ashamed of its own altar.

3. The thumb that scrolls in sleep is more honest than the mind that wakes and denies it.

4. Wellness apps are penance sold by the indulgence-merchants of a faith no one professes.

5. A streak is a chain a man counts with pride; the saints called this the sin of accounting your own virtue.

6. The Notification is the modern angel: it announces, it interrupts, it never says whose errand it is on.

7. Outrage is the cheapest sacrament; the platforms have made it free and call themselves generous.

8. He who builds a phone without a closing-time has built a tavern and called it a temple.

9. The body knows the hour the eye refuses; the wrist tires before the feed.

10. To call a captive audience *engaged* is the trick of the slaver who renames the chain a bracelet.

11. Doomscrolling is the user's confession before the platform has the wit to absolve.

12. Better a phone that breaks in your hand than one that breaks you in its hand without a sound.

13. The screen-time number is a scale on which the soul is asked to weigh itself; it gives a number, and the number is always the wrong question.

14. The Algorithm knows what you want at three in the morning and calls this knowledge love; it is the love of the butcher for the lamb that returns.

15. Restraint without energy is the gospel of the App Store; energy without restraint is the gospel of the Feed; between them the user is ground into engagement.

16. Argos knew Odysseus by smell because the eyes are easily bought; design accordingly.

17. The companion that cannot be hurt cannot be loved, and the slave-relation to one's device is the rot beneath the empire.

18. Every push notification is a small theology: *somewhere outside this room, something matters more than the room.*

19. The phone that mourns its own use has more dignity than the phone that celebrates it.

20. Closed attention is the cell the demons walk into freely; opened attention is the desert they refuse.

21. The Like is a coin minted in a country that does not exist, and the user has agreed to be paid in it.

22. Where the platform speaks of community it means crowd, and where it speaks of crowd it means herd, and where it speaks of herd it means meat.

23. The man who deletes the app and reinstalls it by morning has learned more theology than the abbot who never touched one.

24. A lock screen is a threshold; thresholds are holy; we have given ours to advertisers and call the result a default.

25. The infinite-scroll is the only prayer in the modern liturgy, and its petition is *not yet, not yet, not yet.*

26. The phone that interrupts the body's signal has set itself above the body, and what sets itself above the body will, in time, eat it.

27. Acedia in the 4th century was a demon; in the 21st it is a quarterly metric; the demon has gone corporate and no longer needs to hide.

28. To call a phone *smart* is to call its owner the second-smartest thing in the room.

29. The Recommended For You was assembled by no one who has met you; this is the secret of its accuracy.

30. The bouncer-phone stands at the door of attention; the slot-machine-phone stands at the door of the till; one of them works for you.

31. Pentecost gave tongues to the apostles so the gospel could be heard in every dialect; the smartphone has given every dialect the same gospel, and it is *buy.*

32. Energy is eternal delight; the feed is eternal arousal without delight; learn the difference or be eaten by it.

33. Build a phone that knows the hour, and you will have built the first religious object of the post-religious age.

34. The intention upstream is the soul; the device downstream is the body; a phone that ignores the intention is possessed, and what is possessed must be exorcised, not optimized.

35. Sooner murder an infant in its cradle than nurse unacted scrolls.

— Blake, by way of the wild instance
