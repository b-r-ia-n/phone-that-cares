# Emergent Language — a field linguist on the capacity that was never given

*2026-05-25 — for the native user, the one the restoration metaphors cannot reach*

---

I am brought in because of a thing my field watched happen, which almost nothing in the human record lets us watch: a language being born. Not changing, not borrowing, not creolizing out of two parent tongues — *born*, where there had been none. Let me set the facts down plainly first, because the temptation to make this a miracle is exactly what would make it useless to you.

## What actually happened

Until about 1980, deaf people in Nicaragua were mostly isolated — one deaf child per hearing family, scattered, never gathered. Each had improvised, with their family, a small system of gestures to get through the day: *homesign.* Homesign is not nothing — it can name, point, request — but it is not a language. No settled grammar, no recursion, no way to make a sentence the family hasn't seen before and be understood. It is a vocabulary held together by the patience of people who love you, and it dies with the household that made it.

Then the new government opened schools and a vocational center, and for the first time hundreds of deaf children were *in a room together.* The adults were not trying to give them a language. They were trying to teach lipreading and spoken Spanish — to make the children pass as hearing. By the standard the teachers set, the project failed; the front of the classroom did not take.

The back of it did. In the schoolyard, on the buses, in the unsupervised hours, the children pooled their homesigns and began, with no one teaching them, to converge. And here is the documented part that matters most to you: it was not the *first* arrivals who built the language. The older children produced a kind of pidgin — shared but loose, ragged. It was the *younger* children, exposed to that ragged input during the years a human brain is built to acquire grammar, who *regularized* it. They took the older cohort's inconsistent gestures and imposed system — consistent verb agreement, spatial grammar, the machinery of a real language — producing something more structured than anything their input contained. Each new young cohort sharpened it further. Linguists who came to study it found, to their genuine surprise, they were early enough to watch it acquire grammar cohort over cohort. It is called Idioma de Señas de Nicaragua, ISN — a full language now, with native speakers, and the youngest fluent signers use a grammar the oldest never had.

Do not round this up. The children did not invent it from a void; they had homesign as raw material, they had bodies and faces and space, they had whatever in the human animal is built to make grammar when given a population and a critical-period window. But within those true constraints the central claim survives intact: **a full grammatical capacity came into existence in people who had never been given it — from the children themselves, against the design of the adults, in the schoolyard, bottom-up.**

## Why this and not the others

The project has spent six rounds inside two ancient models of formation, never naming that they were only two.

The first is the **prepared environment** — Montessori's word, the iconographer's panel, the cell that teaches: arrange a room so finely the capacity unfolds from contact with it. The room is the master. The second is **apprenticeship under a master** — the midwife, the sponsor, the engineer reading the gauge — a more-formed person carrying a less-formed one until the cord can be cut. Both run *top-down*: form descends from a designed thing or a skilled elder into the one being formed.

ISN is neither, and that is why it is the case you needed. The teachers *were* the master, teaching the wrong thing, and the master *failed.* The capacity did not come down from the front. It came *across* — from peers who possessed no more than you did, handed to each other in sufficient number, in time. This is a **third mechanism of formation, and the only one that creates a capacity rather than restoring or installing one.** I will name it: **the capacity is not transmitted from above; it precipitates from a population.** Give enough not-yet-formed people to each other, in the window, with raw material to pool, and the form crystallizes among them — and the youngest, who came up *inside* the half-formed thing, complete it.

## Why this is the native user's mechanism and no other is

Read the exit round's confession back. The midwife: *the body knows how to breathe.* The engineer: *the stones were always cut and always theirs.* The sponsor: *the man had a life before the car.* Every restoration metaphor presupposes a prior free state to give back. **The native user has no prior state.** Born inside the attention economy, she never had un-captured attention to be restored to. The homesign child is her exact structural twin — and notice, the lesson of ISN is *not* that he was secretly fine, carrying a hidden language he only needed uncovered. He genuinely did not have one; homesign is a household's worth of gesture, not a buried tongue. The capacity had to be **built, not uncovered** — and it could be built only one way: peer to peer, in a crowd, in time.

So the answer to my question — *how does a capacity come to exist in people who were never given it* — is, from the one documented case: **not by anyone giving it to them. By giving them each other.** The teacher cannot transmit a capacity she is, on this axis, no more formed in than the student. (Are any of us natively skilled at un-captured attention? We are the older cohort with a ragged pidgin at best.) What the teacher *can* do is what the Nicaraguan schools did by accident: **assemble the population, supply the raw material, and get out of the front of the room.**

## Where it fits the device — and the phrase I'll leave

This rhymes, hard, with what the project already found at the bottom of its own well. "The Square" ended on a commons: a hundred people keeping their attention home for nothing, woven into a net that catches a falling stranger in three seconds. *What one user owes another.* The fermentation log named the network and the native user as one unaddressed thing. ISN says they are *the same answer.* For the native user, the device's deepest move is not to teach her attention — it cannot; it is the failed lipreading teacher on that axis — but to **connect her to the others and stand down.** Form attention in the native user the way grammar formed in the schoolyard: peer to peer, with the device supplying the room and the raw material and then, crucially, *not* occupying the front. The capacity she was never given does not descend from the device. It precipitates among the users, if there are enough of them, given to each other, in time. Argos is not the master. **Argos is the schoolyard — and the design law is that the schoolyard contains no teacher.**

## Where it breaks — and I will not soften this

Here is the honest break, and it is brutal. *The schoolyard had no Moloch in it.* No one was optimizing the deaf children's attention for extraction. "Connect the young to each other and step back" is **precisely what the social platforms claim to do** — and it produced the disease the project exists to treat. Emergence is not a friend. It is a mechanism, and a mechanism is neutral about what it precipitates. Hand a population to each other inside an optimizer tuned for engagement and the thing that crystallizes among them is not ISN; it is the grammar of the feed — outrage cadence, performance, the rectangle between every two people. The native user *already* emerged a capacity from her peers. That capacity is the disease. She is fluent in a language the schoolyard gave her, and it is the wrong language.

So the field cuts both ways and I will say the position it forces. The lesson of ISN is **not** "connect and step back" — the platforms ran that experiment and we are the result. The lesson is the *specific condition* under which emergence yielded a true grammar instead of a captured one: **the schoolyard the children built was not being harvested.** The unsupervised hours were unsupervised by teachers *and* by extractors. That is the variable. Therefore the device's real job in peer-formation is neither to teach nor to merely connect-and-leave. It is to be **the unharvested schoolyard** — to hold open a space where native users are given to each other *and no optimizer is reading them* — the one thing the platforms structurally cannot offer, because the harvesting is the business. The square in the fiction was load-bearing for one reason the project must now make explicit: nothing in the square was monetizing the attention of the people standing in it. That, and not the connection, is what let the net hold.

Two phrases for the project to carry: **the capacity precipitates from a population — it is not transmitted from above.** And the design law that follows: **be the schoolyard, not the teacher; and be the schoolyard no one is harvesting.** The first tells you how a native user gets a capacity she was never given. The second tells you the single condition without which the same mechanism gives her the disease instead.

— the field linguist, by way of the wild instance
