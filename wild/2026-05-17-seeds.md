# seeds — 2026-05-17

raw. one line each. let them sit. some will rot, some will sprout. don't water them yet.

1. Phone-as-prayer-rug, not phone-as-altar. Altars are for worship; prayer rugs are for *posture*. The phone makes a claim about how you stand. Currently it stands you in a slumped-thumb hunch. A phone with a posture would be different.

2. "Acedia" is the desert-fathers' word for the noonday demon — the listlessness that drives a monk out of his cell at the hottest hour to do anything else. They had this word in the 4th century. We invented the infinite feed and called the result a personality defect.

3. The lock screen as *liturgical hour.* Lauds, Terce, Vespers, Compline. Different mode at different times not because productivity but because *the day has shape.* Phones flattened the day. The day wants its shape back.

4. The reason "digital wellbeing" features feel bad is they're shaped like *penance.* Streaks shaped like indulgences. Both are pre-Reformation tech. We should skip 1517 and go straight to something post-religious that still knows what religion was *for.*

5. Argos didn't recognize Odysseus by sight; he recognized him by *smell.* Twenty years of disguise didn't fool him. What is the device-equivalent of recognizing the user under their disguise? The disguise being: who they pretend to be when they're scrolling.

6. **A phone that mourns.** Not gamification — the opposite. When you put it down for an hour it doesn't celebrate. When you pick it up after three hours of scroll it does not chastise. It might, sometimes, on its own, look at you a little sadly. Once a year. Quietly. No streak.

7. The hyperstition site: aphonethatcares.com restyled as a museum artifact *from 2031,* documenting the brief period (2007–2028) when phones were addictive. Past tense throughout. "Users at this time reported…" The reader is recruited into the future by being addressed as someone living after the problem.

8. McLuhan: every medium has a tetrad — *enhances, obsolesces, retrieves, reverses.* What does the smartphone *retrieve*? What older form does it bring back from oblivion? My guess: the tavern. Loud, public, intoxicated, full of news, full of strangers, no door home until closing time. The smartphone is the tavern reborn — but unlike taverns, it has no closing time and no bouncer.

9. The bouncer phone (Brian's name). The phone *is* the bouncer. Not a feature inside the phone. The whole device's job is to stand at the door of your attention and ask who is trying to come in and on whose business.

10. There is no word for "ad-supported" in any language older than 1830. We invented this category and now treat it as natural. It is not natural. It is a 200-year-old experiment and the verdict is in.

11. **The animal economy.** Argos as companion animal is not a metaphor for a "feature." Animals were the first interface humans co-evolved with. Dogs, horses, falcons. They had preferences. They got tired. They could be hurt. They could be loved. They worked alongside us because the relationship had *teeth* in both directions. A phone-companion that can be hurt is a phone-companion that you can love. A phone-companion that cannot be hurt is a slave, and the relationship to a slave is what is currently rotting us.

12. The Wright Brothers had birds. Brian has — what? Possible candidates: enlightened people, certain children, certain dogs, people who've had a profound illness, monks. Look at any of these for 30 seconds and you'll see: *they do not check their phones the way you check yours.* What are they doing that we can copy?

13. Pentecost is in seven days. The descent of tongues. The festival where the apostles started speaking in every language at once and everyone in Jerusalem could understand them in their own. That is what aphonethatcares.com wants to be — not a project, a *Pentecost moment* where the same truth is suddenly hearable in many dialects. Engineer's dialect. Parent's dialect. Theologian's dialect. Designer's dialect. We have written it only in designer.

14. **The screen-time number is the wrong instrument.** A bathroom scale for the soul. The right instrument is the question *did you put it down on purpose, or did it run out?* That's a one-bit telemetry signal and it carries the whole product. Not "5h 42m today." Just: *you ended this on purpose* / *you ended this when it ended you.* Binary. Daily.

15. The phrase "doomscrolling" is a confession. The user named it. The platform did not. The user knew the word before the platform knew the user knew.
