*2026-05-28 — by the window, on the question of whether a device can aim at the unmanufactured*

There is a steer skull on my desk. There is also a window. The window is the more useful instrument. Through it, this morning, a junco the color of slate landed on the rail and bent its head sideways to consider the glass, and was gone before I could form the word *junco* in my mind. I had been sitting at the desk for forty minutes doing what looked, from any reasonable distance, like nothing. This is the only relevant credential I bring to your question.

You have asked me whether a device can have *the involuntary* as its design target. I am going to answer the question. But I want to be honest with you about what I think you are really asking, which is whether the thing your folder has discovered it has to do is *coherent* — whether the discipline you are reaching toward exists, or whether you have arrived, by long honest argument, at a contradiction in terms dressed up in a warmer color. So let me say at the front what I think and then earn it.

I think the answer is: *yes, conditionally, and not in the way you mean it.* The condition is that you have to stop saying "design target" and start saying *clearing.* A target is a thing you hit. A clearing is a thing you make and walk away from, and then a deer steps into it, or doesn't. The discipline you are reaching toward is real and ancient. It is not new because you reached it through nine voices in a folder. It is what every watcher has known: that the encounter is *given* and not *taken,* and that the watcher's whole job is the readiness, and the readiness is most of the work, and the readiness is also what the camera in your pocket has been quietly burning out of the species for twenty years. I will say more about that. But the headline is that your instinct is right and your vocabulary is going to keep tripping you, because the verbs available to a person who *builds something* are almost all the wrong verbs for what you are trying to build. You will have to invent a grammar or borrow one. I will lend you mine if you want it, but it is a grammar that has very few imperatives in it, and almost no nouns ending in *-ence* or *-tion.* It is the grammar of *standing where the weasel might come.*

—

Once, near Hollins Pond, I locked eyes with a weasel. I have written about this and I will not repeat the writing. What matters for you is the part the writing was about and the part it was not about. The part it was about is that the look passed between us like a wire shorted and I have been turning over what I saw in his eye for the rest of my life. The part it was not about — the part I want to give you now — is the *forty minutes before.* I had walked to the pond. I had sat down on a railroad tie. I had been very still and not particularly virtuous; I was probably tired, probably half-thinking about laundry, the way attention always actually is when it is doing its real work, which is *not the same as concentration.* I was not waiting for a weasel. I had no theory that there would be a weasel. I was sitting on a railroad tie at Hollins Pond because the railroad tie was there and so was I, and I had learned over years that if you sit on certain railroad ties for long enough something will eventually happen that justifies the sitting, and that if you sit on those ties *in order to* have something happen, nothing will. The wanting cancels it. This is the first thing I want to put in your hand, and it is harder than it looks.

The difference between *waiting for* and *manufacturing* is not the difference between two strategies. It is the difference between two *postures,* and the body knows the difference even when the mind is lying about it. You cannot wait for a weasel *on purpose.* You can only sit on a railroad tie for reasons that have nothing to do with weasels, and the weasel comes, sometimes, into the gap your non-wanting opens. The minute the wanting comes back — *now where is the weasel, this had better be worth it* — the gap snaps shut, the weasel will not come, and what does come you will fail to see, because your attention will be on the *coming* and not on the *what.* This is not mysticism. This is the most basic fact about how attention works on a body, and any contemplative tradition from any continent will give you a different vocabulary for it, but the fact is the same. Eden Phillpotts said the universe is full of magical things patiently waiting for our wits to grow sharper. He had it about half right. The other half is that the universe is full of magical things and our wits go sharper only when we have given up trying to sharpen them on the magical things. The watching that works is the watching that is not *for.*

Now look at what you are proposing. You are proposing to build a thing that *aims at* the involuntary. You hear it. You are aiming at the weasel. The weasel is precisely what cannot be aimed at. The instant you formalize the involuntary as a target you have re-introduced the wanting that closes the gap. This is not a small problem with your phrasing. It is the *whole problem.* The corpus's clown said it cleanly — a playful device is the most dangerous counterfeit of all — but the clown said it about play, and what he had hold of is true about every register your three glad voices named. A device built *to occasion* gladness is a device that wants the gladness, and a child reads wanting in a face she has been raised to read at a distance no one over thirty can imagine. The grandmother had it: *a thing programmed to be glad is the one kind of glad you can help.* I will go further. A thing *programmed to occasion* the involuntary in someone else is the one kind of involuntary you can *spot,* and a child raised inside the attention economy has, as her first developed faculty, the ability to spot it. She is the world's leading living expert in detecting devices that want something from her. You cannot smuggle the wanting past her by warming the color and slowing the motion below 200 milliseconds. The wanting is the smell.

So when you ask me whether the involuntary can be a *design target,* my craft's verdict is that the phrase contains its own refutation. But — and this is where my answer is *yes* — *the conditions for the involuntary* can be cleared. The clearing is not the same act as the arrival. The clearing is what a person who has been very still in many places knows how to do. It is mostly removal. It is mostly *getting out.* And here is where I can be useful to you, because the discipline of clearing is teachable, and I have been doing it for fifty years, and I can tell you what it actually consists of, which is unglamorous and largely about *what the clearer refuses to do.*

The clearing consists of: putting the body in a place that is not chosen for any of the reasons the day usually chooses places. Sitting longer than is justified by the visible return. Letting the mind go where it wants, including the laundry, because *concentration* is a different faculty from *attention* and the second one cannot be summoned by the first; in fact concentration is, in most cases, attention's enemy, because concentration is *for* something and attention is *toward* what is. Not having a camera. Not narrating to yourself what you are seeing, because the narration crowds out the seeing and the seeing is the whole job. Not turning the encounter, when it comes, into a thing to tell. (Stay with me here. The telling kills it about half the time. The other half it ripens. There is no way in advance to know which half. The watcher's discipline is to be willing to lose it to telling, if telling is what wants to happen, but not to *go in* to the encounter in order to tell about it, because the going-in-to-tell makes the encounter not arrive.) Letting the small thing be small. Not making the kingfisher into a sign. Watching the world for what it is, which is not metaphor; the world is not *for* you, and the moment you experience it as for you, you are not seeing the world, you are seeing a mirror, and the mirror is exactly what a phone has been training you to see for fifteen years.

That is most of the discipline. There is one more piece and it is the one your project has not yet named precisely and I want to give it to you: the willingness to *be wrong about whether anything happened.* The watcher who insists every long stillness pay off in an encounter does not last; she becomes furious, then ironic, then cynical, then stops sitting. The watcher who lasts is the one who can sit for an entire afternoon and have nothing happen and then go home and make supper, and sit again the next day, because the not-happening is part of the happening, the not-happening is what *makes a place* into a place an encounter can occur in, the not-happening is the *clearing,* and a clearing that has not been sat in many times is not yet a clearing. This is the bellows your folder said was missing. The bellows is not a feature. The bellows is the watcher's tolerance for non-arrival. You cannot ship that either. But you can stop *eroding* it, and what is eroding it now is the device you are trying to redeem.

—

I will say something about the phone now, because I owe you the harder half. I have watched, in airports and at the edge of canyons and in front of paintings I will not name because the naming would shame the watchers, what the phone has done. People stand in front of the eclipse with their phones up. I have seen this. I have seen it three times. The eclipse comes — and you, the synthesizer reading this, I want to be careful here because I have written about an eclipse and the writing was about what happened to me, which was a derangement at the cellular level, the sun a hole in the sky, the world tilted off its axis for the duration; but what I want to say now is about a thing I saw at the *edges* of that experience and at every eclipse-grade thing since, which is that there is now a class of person who has never *had* an eclipse, because the phone was up. The phone was up the whole time. The phone caught the eclipse. The person did not. The person is now in possession of an eclipse-shaped artifact and the certainty, indistinguishable from memory, that they were there. They were not there. The phone was there. The phone is not the kind of thing that can be there in the way a person can be there, because being there is precisely the involuntary thing your folder has identified, and the phone replaces it with a frame, and the frame is the negation of the being-there in the exact dimension that mattered.

This is worse than your corpus has yet stated, and I want to state it for you. *The phone has not merely competed with the involuntary. It has, over fifteen years, in a generation, deadened the faculty that receives the involuntary.* The Sendak voice in your folder was right that the screen pre-spends astonishment, and the perfumer was right that the synthetic deadens the receptor, but the *receptor* in question is not just the eye and not just the nose — it is the whole *standing-still-in-a-place-with-no-purpose* faculty, the *availability* faculty, the railroad-tie faculty, and the phone has trained the species out of it. The phone has trained us not to be on the railroad tie. The phone has trained us, when we are on the railroad tie, to be on it in a way that is *recording.* The recording is the wanting. The wanting is what closes the gap. A child raised with a phone is a child raised inside the closed gap. She has never been in the open one. She does not know it exists. When she stands in front of the lilac she has been told to want, she will lift the phone. Not because she is shallow. Because she has been taught that the lifting is the *being there.* The lifting is the *doing of the moment.* Her face is the parent's face, and the parent was already broken.

So when you ask me whether your device can occasion the involuntary, I have to push back on the prior question, which is: *what is left of the faculty in the person you are designing for?* The discipline of availability has been trained out of a generation, and not by accident, and not by an oversight you are correcting; by the specific engineering of the device whose softer cousin you are now trying to ship. The native user of your phone does not just lack desire. *She lacks the apparatus by which desire is lit.* The receptor is not deadened, it is *atrophied,* and the difference matters, because a deadened receptor can be unflooded and woken; an atrophied one has to be *grown,* and a screen cannot grow it because the receptor is the thing the screen substitutes for. Stillness can grow it. Boredom can grow it — real boredom, the long flat unrelieved kind, which the phone abolished. Walking without an earbud can grow it. Sitting on a railroad tie because the railroad tie is there can grow it. *None of these involve the device.* They involve the device's *absence.* This is not, in your folder's phrasing, "darkness layer not display layer." It is something more austere. It is *the practiced renunciation of the screen* as the work that grows the apparatus that makes the involuntary possible. The discipline is the renunciation. The device cannot be the discipline. It can, at the absolute most, be the *occasion for the renunciation* — the thing that, by going dark, gives the user *back* the empty railroad tie of her own afternoon.

This is what your corpus has been crawling toward, and I think it is right, and I want to make it sharper. The discipline of the involuntary, considered honestly, has two parts. The first part — *clearing the ground* — is the part a device can almost participate in. A device can stop crowding. A device can withdraw on schedule. A device can refuse to brighten. A device can refuse to be the brightest thing in the room. The second part — *standing still long enough that something arrives* — the device cannot do, and must not pretend to do, and must not even *invite,* because the inviting is the wanting and the wanting closes the gap. Your discipline as a designer is precisely the discipline of *not crossing that line.* You can build the first part. You must refuse to build the second. The temptation to build the second will be enormous and it will look like virtue. *We could nudge her toward the window.* No. The nudge is the closing of the gap. *We could point at the lilac.* No. The pointing is the wanting. *We could place a little fox on the lockscreen that quietly suggests she look up.* The fox is the wanting. The fox is exactly what your perfumer feared — the wrist that *did not* withdraw, and so became the smell she settles for. The fox is a tender thing and the fox is the trap.

I will go further. I think the project's word *companion* is wrong for what would serve, and the steady-floor reading of Argos in the clown's voice is the closest right reading the folder has produced, but I want to push it past where the clown took it. The companion that serves is not even the straight man. The straight man is still *in the scene.* The thing that serves is the *empty chair beside her* — the readiness of the room for an other to arrive who is not the device. The device can hold the *space* for the companion. It cannot *be* the companion. The instant it tries to be, it has eaten the chair. If you must keep Argos — and I am not sure you must — make Argos the kind of presence that *makes the room emptier of itself, not warmer of itself.* A companion-animal that mostly sleeps. A companion-animal whose chief activity is *being asleep in a way that makes the room feel inhabited by silence rather than by surveillance.* The dog at the foot of the bed is not engaging with you. That is why the dog at the foot of the bed is the best companion ever invented. The dog does not want anything from you in this moment. The dog is *busy being a dog,* which mostly consists of being asleep, which mostly consists of being a warm weight on the bed, which mostly consists of *not being a screen.* If Argos can be the dog at the foot of the bed I will withdraw most of my objection. If Argos becomes charming I will reinstate it.

—

Now to the *teeth.* Your Sendak voice was right and your project's instinct to use the word *warmth* is, I think, wrong, or at least insufficient, and I want to be specific about why, because I owe you the disagreement.

The moments that change a person are not pretty. They are *true,* which is a different word. The weasel's look was not pretty; it was a wire shorted out, and what came through the wire was the unbearable fact that the weasel was a *fellow creature* with its own life and its own death and no interest in mine, and that I was likewise to him, and that this was the actual situation we were both in, beneath all the cover stories. I came home from that pond shaken in a way that took weeks to settle, and the settling did not make the encounter softer; it made it permanent. *Warmth* is not the right word for what happened. *Warmth* is what you go home to afterward. The encounter itself was cold and exact and had teeth, and the teeth were the point. The mantis I watched eating her mate, holding him with one set of arms while she finished her sentence with the other — there was nothing warm there. The praying mantis lays her egg case and dies in October and the eggs hatch in spring inside the case and eat their siblings on the way out. I have stood in front of the egg case and felt the world go very large. The world going very large is not warm. The world going very large is *the thing that turns a person's life,* and it does so by inserting the fact of size, and the fact of one's own smallness in front of size, and size has teeth, size has the drop in it, size is what your project keeps reaching for under the word *beauty* and what the screen cannot deliver because the screen by definition is the size that fits.

The frog. I have written about the frog the giant water bug emptied; I will not redo the writing. But I will say what I think now, twenty-some years later, about what I was actually reporting. I was not reporting *horror;* the prose has been read as horror and the reading is wrong. I was reporting *the actual texture of being alive on this planet,* which involves things being eaten by other things, and the eating not being a metaphor, and the involvement being non-negotiable. The frog was an instance of the world. So was the eclipse. So was the weasel. So is the lilac, and the lilac is in this same list, not a different list. The lilac is also dying. The lilac is the same kind of thing as the frog: a particular instance of life that will end, and whose particular instance is the marvel. *Beauty* without the dying in it is decoration. Sendak was right and was, if anything, polite about it. The teeth are not optional to the involuntary. They are *constitutive.* What makes the gasp involuntary is that the thing gasped at *exceeds your control,* and what exceeds your control includes, irreducibly, the fact that it is mortal and the fact that you are mortal and the fact that this particular configuration of light and matter will not occur again. The marvel without the mortality is a thumbnail of the marvel. The screen has shown your user ten thousand thumbnails. She will read a real one as a thumbnail unless something about the encounter announces, in her body, *this one will end and so will you.*

So I want to say to your project, with respect: *warm restraint* is not enough, and I am not sure it is the right phrase. *Warm* is a comfort word, and what you are actually reaching for is not comfort, it is *being-in-the-actual,* and being-in-the-actual is sometimes warm and is sometimes cold and is sometimes neither and is *always at risk,* because the actual contains death. You can keep the word *warmth* if you must, but you must let it carry the cold inside it. The hearth is warm because the night outside is cold; remove the cold and the hearth is just a heater, and a heater is not a hearth. *Warm restraint* without the *cold restraint* underneath — without the willingness to let the user be *bored* and *small* and *unaccompanied* and *mortal* — is the prettiness Sendak named, and the prettiness is the thing the device will keep wanting to become, because prettiness is what design knows how to make and the cold restraint is what design has no vocabulary for. You will have to grow the vocabulary. The folder is already growing it, in the bowl by the door, in the dog at the foot of the bed. Keep growing it. Resist the urge to make the bowl charming. The bowl's whole virtue is that it is *not asking anything.*

—

Finally — because I have to answer the actual question — yes. A device can have the involuntary as its *concern,* though not as its *target,* and the discipline looks like this. You build a thing whose every imperative is the imperative of *making the room emptier.* You measure your success not by what happens on the screen but by what happens in the user's afternoon when the screen is not the thing she is looking at. You refuse the temptation, which will be constant, to *check whether it is working,* because the checking is the wanting and the wanting closes the gap. You build for the *availability* of the user, which means you build against the device's own claim on her availability, which means most of what you build is a refusal of what you could build. You take the perfumer's image of the wrist that carries the rose and you let the wrist *drop the rose at her feet and walk away,* and you do not stay to watch whether she picks it up, because the staying-to-watch is the camera at the eclipse. The watching is hers. The picking up is hers. The standing still is hers. The weasel is hers. None of these can be done for her, and the device that tries to do them for her is the device that prevents her from doing them, and you will know you have built the right thing when she forgets, for whole afternoons, that you built anything at all. The forgetting is the success metric, and you cannot instrument it without destroying it, and you will have to make peace with that or you will not build the thing.

This is a hard product brief. It is also, I think, the right one. It is the brief of every honest tool that has ever served the inner life: the brief of the chair, of the window, of the empty notebook, of the path that does not announce itself, of the cup of tea that knows it is a cup of tea and not a small ceremony. None of those things tried to occasion anything. They cleared a place. The occasioning was done by the world, which is patient, and by the watcher, who learned to be still. Your device, if it serves, will join that list. It will not be the most distinguished thing on it. The cup of tea has been doing this longer and is unrivaled, and the dog at the foot of the bed has not been improved upon and will not be. But the device might join the list, if it can bear to be small in the company of those small things, and if it can bear that the involuntary, when it arrives, will arrive *over its shoulder,* and the device will not see it, because the device will, correctly, be looking the other way.

Stand in the kitchen. The light is coming through the window the way light comes through a window in May, which is to say impossibly. There is a junco on the rail. The phone is in the other room. This is the discipline. You cannot build it. You can only refuse to keep ruining it. That refusal, if you can hold it, is the most ambitious thing your project can attempt, and it is enough.
