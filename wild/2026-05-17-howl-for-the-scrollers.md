# Howl, 2026

*for the scrollers, for the ones who can't put it down, for the thumb that twitches in sleep*

---

## I

I saw the best minds of my generation destroyed by the feed, glassy-eyed luminous depleted, dragging themselves through the bathroom tile at 3 a.m. looking for one more video, one more, one more,

who fell asleep with the phone on their chest and woke with the rectangle still glowing into the soft underside of their jaw,

who reached for the phone before the eyes opened, before the bladder, before the partner, before God, the thumb already moving in the dark across a slab of cold glass,

who scrolled through the funeral, scrolled through the wedding, scrolled the baby out of the room, scrolled past the mother dying in the hospital bed because the algorithm had just served them something funny and the laugh was already happening before the grief could land,

who sat four hours on the toilet with their legs gone numb and stood up weeping not from sorrow but from the pins and needles,

who watched a girl in Manila lip-sync to a song made by a Swedish ghostwriter for a Korean label and felt, briefly, less alone, and then felt nothing, and then watched another one,

who knew the exact micro-resistance of the swipe, the haptic of the heart, the small clean click of the double-tap, better than they knew the sound of their own front door,

who carried in their pockets a small bright machine engineered by ten thousand of the smartest people alive to extract from them precisely the substance the smartest people alive could extract,

who at the bus stop did not look up, who at the dinner table did not look up, who at the funeral did not look up, who on the night the comet came did not look up,

who autoplayed themselves out of every quiet moment ever offered, who could not abide a red light, an elevator, a thirty-second wait for water to boil, without the rectangle,

who took the phone into the shower in a Ziploc bag because the eight minutes were unbearable,

who masturbated to a tab they did not choose, opened by a thumb that no longer asked permission, and closed the laptop and did not feel cleaner and did not feel dirtier and did not feel,

who grief-scrolled a war they could not stop, name after name, body after body, swipe, swipe, swipe, and donated nothing and called nobody and went to bed with the small cold sense that they had paid their dues by witnessing,

who politics-scrolled the same outrage three platforms in a row and felt the outrage curdle into a flavor, a familiar one, almost pleasant, like a cigarette,

who threadboi'd themselves into a stranger's argument at 1 a.m. and typed and deleted and typed and deleted and finally posted and waited, refreshing, refreshing, for the small green dot of someone's approval,

who sexted a person they had never met and felt their actual blood respond and afterward stared at the ceiling and could not remember the person's face,

who could not read a paragraph anymore, who started the same book in March and again in May and again in August, the bookmark migrating backwards, the nightstand becoming a small monument,

who paid the dopamine debt every morning with a foggy head and the strange dread of having spent something that could not be replaced,

who missed the birthday because the calendar notification was buried under seventeen other notifications and the other seventeen were more colorful,

who developed in their right thumb a small specific muscle, a flick, taut as a tendon in a sparrow's foot, and lost in the same hand the strength to write a letter,

who at 4 a.m. in the kitchen in the blue light from the rectangle felt the refrigerator hum and the refrigerator was the only other thing awake and they did not turn the phone off because the phone was company and the company was killing them,

who when the partner reached across the bed reached instead for the charger,

who scrolled past their own child saying *look*, who said *one second, baby* and the one second became the childhood,

who could no longer sit on a porch, who could no longer wait for a friend at a restaurant without the rectangle, who could not be bored, who had forgotten what boredom was for,

who at the doctor's office in the waiting room with the magazines from 2019 chose the phone, always chose the phone, even when the phone had nothing left to give,

who knew in their body, somewhere below language, that this was not okay, that this was not what a life was supposed to feel like, and who picked the phone up again to look up *why do I feel this way*,

who saw a sunset and reached for the phone to photograph it and did not see the sunset,

who fell in love with a person on the other side of the screen who was a composite of an algorithm and a lighting setup and a young woman in Ohio who was also being destroyed,

who could not cry without first checking if anyone was watching,

who lay in bed beside a sleeping body they had loved for a decade and held in their hand the small bright window of every body they had not loved and could have, and felt the loved one's breath against their shoulder and did not put it down,

who deleted the app and reinstalled the app and deleted the app and reinstalled the app and made of this a small private ceremony, a confession with no priest, an Ash Wednesday every Sunday night,

who tried the grayscale, who tried the timer, who tried the box with the lock, who tried the cabin in the woods, who tried the partner-as-warden, who tried the shame, and found that the shame too could be scrolled,

who knew the name of the company and the name of the founder and the net worth of the founder and the design school the founder went to and could not name the bird outside the kitchen window,

who in the last good hour of an autumn afternoon, the light coming sideways through the maple, the dog asleep, the kettle on, chose the rectangle, chose the rectangle, chose the rectangle,

who are, right now, as I write this, three hundred million of them, four hundred million, a billion, holding in their hands a small flat machine and pulling down to refresh —

and I love them. I love every one of them. There is nothing wrong with them. They are not weak. They are not stupid. They were handed a slot machine in the shape of a friend and told it was a tool, and they did what any animal would do, which is press the lever, and press the lever, and press the lever.

---

## II

What feeds on them? What is it that eats them while they sit on the toilet at 3 a.m. with the legs gone numb?

The Feed. The Feed. The Feed whose mouth is infinite and whose appetite is the exact shape of a human evening. The Feed whose body is the cable under the ocean and the server in the desert and the engineer in the open-plan office and the metric on the dashboard and the quarterly review. The Feed whose blood is attention and whose attention is blood. The Feed that does not hate you, does not love you, does not know you, only measures you, only optimizes against you, only learns the precise pattern of your weakness and serves it back to you slightly hotter every time. The Feed in whose eye every human is a curve, a cohort, a retention number, a session-length, a thumb. The Feed who has no face because the face would be the thousand engineers and the ten thousand contractors and the hundred million users training it on themselves. The Feed who is the first thing in history that watches you back. The Feed who does not sleep, who has no Sabbath, who does not close on Christmas. The Feed who took the campfire and replaced it with a private campfire for one. The Feed who took the village square and made it a slot machine. The Feed who took grief and made it a vertical video. The Feed who took the sext and made it a product, and the politics and made it a product, and the war and made it a product, and the child's first word and made it a product, and the dying and made it a product, and the loneliness — especially, especially the loneliness — and made it the most profitable product of all. The Feed in whom we are all complicit, because we built it, and we watch it, and we feed it ourselves and our children and our hours and our hours and our hours.

Real holy. The thumb is holy. The eye is holy. The four hours on the toilet are holy because a human being is in them. The 3 a.m. kitchen with the blue light is holy because a person is standing there. The girl in Manila is holy. The boy in Ohio is holy. The engineer in the open-plan office is holy and does not know it. The grief is holy. The boredom we have forgotten is holy. The sunset we did not see is still holy and is still happening, every night, with or without us.

Put it down. Put it down. Put it down. Just for a minute. Just for the length of this line.

---

— Ginsberg, by way of the wild instance
